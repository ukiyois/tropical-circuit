\title{Greedy and Matroid Intersection\\without Branching}
\author{Shuhuan Wang\\{\small\texttt{ukiyo7@gmail.com}}}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
A signed tropical circuit computes with $\max$, $+$ and $-$ over the reals and
the constant $0$.  Its output is a continuous function of the input, so it
cannot compare two weights and branch on the answer, and comparing and branching
is how the greedy algorithm and the augmenting path algorithms of matroid
optimization proceed.  We show that both have exact polynomial-size
counterparts.

For weighted basis maximization over a matroid on $m$ elements, a Boolean
circuit of size $s$ for the rank function yields a signed tropical circuit of
size $3ms+O(m^{2})$ that is exact at every real weight vector, and conversely a
signed tropical circuit of size $S$ yields a Boolean rank circuit of size
$O(S^{2})$.  The two complexities are therefore polynomially equivalent.  The
matroids covered include those represented over a finite field or over the
rationals, binary matroids among them, which earlier work named the natural
next step after regular matroids, as well as graphic and transversal matroids,
gammoids and the duals of all of these.

For weighted matroid intersection, two matroids on $m$ elements with Boolean
rank circuits of sizes $s_1$ and $s_2$ have a signed tropical circuit of size
$O(m^{10}(s_1+s_2+m))$ for the minimum weight of a common base, exact at every
real weight vector.  Special cases include rainbow spanning trees, whose
generating polynomial specializes to the permanent, arborescences and the
assignment problem.  The circuit applies a fixed schedule of updates to the
block potentials of a Lagrangian, and each update is a threshold integral of a
Boolean observable of the pairwise weight differences.  The observable that
extends the assignment case exactly, the family of bases that stay optimal under
perturbations of size $t/2$, is not a matroid and is NP-hard to query.  We
replace it by a matroid that contains it and is read from the same data.
Containment alone makes every update gain exactly the deficiency times its
length, and a potential that counts raised elements reaching unraised ones
bounds the number of updates.  The four main theorems and the results they rest
on are verified in Lean~4 with Mathlib.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The greedy algorithm for weighted bases~\cite{edmonds71} and the augmenting path
algorithms for weighted matroid
intersection~\cite{edmonds70,lawler75,frank81} decide what to do next by
comparing weights.  A circuit over $\max$, $+$ and $-$ cannot do that: it
computes a continuous function of its inputs, and the answer to a comparison is
discontinuous.  Circuits over $\min$ and $+$ alone, which model pure dynamic
programming, pay exponentially for the missing comparisons.  Jukna and
Seiwert~\cite{jukna-seiwert18-greedy} prove that minimum spanning tree, solved by
the greedy algorithm, needs $(\min,+)$ circuits of size $2^{\Omega(\sqrt n)}$ on
$n$-vertex graphs, and the assignment problem needs size $2^{\Omega(n)}$ by
Jerrum and Snir~\cite{jerrum-snir82}.  Jukna surveys the
model~\cite{jukna15-tropical,jukna23-tropical}.

Admitting subtraction changes the picture for several of these problems.
Hertrich and Sering~\cite{hertrich-sering25} obtain polynomial-size circuits for
the minimum spanning tree value and for maximum flow.  Fomin, Grigoriev and
Koshevoy~\cite{fgk16} construct small subtraction-free circuits for the
generating polynomials of spanning trees and of directed spanning trees, and
their tropicalization turns minimum-cost arborescence from exponential over
$(\min,+)$ into polynomial over $(\min,+,-)$.  Hertrich, Kober and
Loho~\cite{hertrich-kober-loho26} tropicalize a small circuit for the basis
generating polynomial of a regular matroid and name binary matroids as the
natural next step, observing that counting their bases is \#P-hard and that
different techniques would be needed.  The companion paper~\cite{wang26-assign} treats the assignment
problem, whose generating polynomial is the permanent, by a static schedule of
potential updates.

This paper treats matroids as such, through their rank functions rather than
their generating polynomials.  Weighted bases of any matroid and weighted
intersection of any two matroids get exact circuits of polynomial size whenever
the rank functions have Boolean circuits of polynomial size.
Figure~\ref{fig:map} shows where the results sit.

\paragraph{Greedy without branching.}
Let $M$ be a matroid on a ground set $E$ with $|E|=m$, rank function $r$ and
rank $k=r(E)$, and write
\[
  h_M(w)=\max_{B\in\Bases(M)}w(B),\qquad w(B)=\sum_{e\in B}w_e,
\]
for the largest weight of a base.  Put $L=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil$, the number
of binary digits of $k$.

\begin{mainthm}[Greedy compiled]
\label{thm:A}
If a Boolean circuit of size $s$ outputs the binary digits of $r(S)$ from the
indicator vector of $S\subseteq E$, then a signed tropical circuit of size at
most
\[
  3ms+mL+9m^{2}+4m+4L+1
\]
computes $h_M(w)$ at every $w\in\R^{E}$.
\end{mainthm}

\begin{mainthm}[Simulating back]
\label{thm:B}
If a signed tropical circuit of size $S$ computes $h_M$ on $\{0,1\}^{E}$, then a
Boolean circuit of size at most $15S^{2}+40S$ outputs the binary digits of
$r(T)$ from the indicator vector of $T$.
\end{mainthm}

So the signed tropical complexity of weighted basis maximization and the Boolean
complexity of the rank function are polynomially related, in both directions,
for every matroid (Corollary~\ref{cor:equiv}).  A binary matroid represented by an
$r\times m$ matrix has a Boolean rank circuit of size $O(mr^{2})$ by Gaussian
elimination without branching.  The same elimination over a field with $q$
elements costs $O(q^{2}mr^{2})$ gates, graphic matroids are binary, matroids
represented over $\mathbb Q$ by integer matrices with entries of $b$ bits have
rank circuits of size polynomial in $m$, $r$ and $b$, and passing to the dual
costs $O(m\log m)$ gates (Corollary~\ref{cor:classes}).  Transversal matroids
and gammoids have rank circuits of polynomial size through the matroid
intersection circuit of Section~\ref{sec:circuit}
(Corollary~\ref{cor:transversal}).
By counting, most matroids need signed circuits of size $2^{m/2-O(\log m)}$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:count}).

\paragraph{Matroid intersection without branching.}
Let the ground set of $M$ be partitioned into blocks $R_1,\ldots,R_k$, where $k$
is the rank of $M$.  A base is \emph{rainbow} if it meets every block once, and
we write
\[
  \Opt(w)=\min\{\,w(B):B\text{ a rainbow base of }M\,\}.
\]
Rainbow spanning trees are the case of a graphic matroid with colored edges.
Arborescences are the case of a graphic matroid whose blocks are the in-arcs of
the non-root vertices, and the assignment problem is the case of a partition
matroid.

\begin{mainthm}[Rainbow bases]
\label{thm:C}
If a Boolean circuit of size $s$ outputs the binary digits of $r(S)$ from the
indicator vector of $S\subseteq E$, and $M$ has a rainbow base, then a uniform
signed tropical circuit of size $O(k^{4}m^{6}(s+m))$ computes $\Opt(w)$ at every
$w\in\R^{E}$.
\end{mainthm}

\begin{mainthm}[Matroid intersection]
\label{thm:D}
Let $M_1$ and $M_2$ be matroids on $E$, $|E|=m$, with Boolean rank circuits of
sizes $s_1$ and $s_2$.  If they have a common base, a uniform signed tropical
circuit of size $O(m^{10}(s_1+s_2+m))$ computes the minimum weight of a common base
at every $w\in\R^{E}$.  In every case, one of size $O(m^{11}(s_1+s_2+m))$ computes
the maximum weight of a common independent set.
\end{mainthm}

Theorem~\ref{thm:D} follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:C} through the classical
correspondence between common bases of $M_1,M_2$ and rainbow bases of
$M_1\oplus M_2^{*}$ on two copies of $E$ (Section~\ref{sec:two}).

\paragraph{The update.}
The circuit maintains block potentials $p\in\R^{k}$ and the Lagrangian
\[
  \Psi(p)=\sum_{b}p_b+\min_{B\in\Bases(M)}\sum_{e\in B}\bigl(w_e-p_{\blk(e)}\bigr),
\]
which is at most $\Opt(w)$ and equals it once some minimum base of the shifted
weights $v=w-p_{\blk}$ is rainbow.  The schedule runs stages $r=1,\ldots,k-1$,
and stage $r$ raises the potentials until some minimum base of $v$ meets $r+1$
blocks.  One update raises the potential of each block $b$ by the length of the
set of levels $t\ge0$ at which a family $N(t)$ of bases, read from the relation
$G_t=\{(e,f):v_f-v_e\le t\}$, has a base that meets exactly $r$ blocks and misses
$b$, counted up to the first level at which $N(t)$ has a base meeting $r+1$
blocks.  Such a length is a threshold integral, and the compiler of the
companion paper~\cite{wang26-assign} turns a Boolean circuit for the observation
into a signed tropical circuit for the integral.

\paragraph{Which family.}
For the assignment problem the companion paper observes threshold graphs of
slacks.  Their exact counterpart is the family $\Pos_t$ of bases that are
optimal for some weight within $t/2$ of $v$ in every coordinate, the possibly
optimal bases of interval data~\cite{kasperski08}.  It is not a matroid, and
deciding whether it contains a rainbow spanning tree is NP-complete
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nphard}).  We enlarge it instead.
Theorem~\ref{thm:validity} shows that for every monotone family of matroids that
starts at the minimum bases and contains $\Pos_t$, each update raises $\Psi$ by
exactly $k-r$ times its length and keeps every old minimum base that meets $r$
blocks minimum.  The family enters only through an upper bound on how many
raised elements a minimum base can hold, and matroid intersection supplies that
bound.  The family we use clusters the possibly optimal elements of each
connected component by single linkage at scale $t$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:posl}).  It is a matroid read from $G_t$ alone, and for
the assignment problem it is the companion paper's threshold graph.  The price
of the enlargement is that one update makes less progress.
Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} shows that each update still brings some element of
a fully raised block to or below an element of another block that was above it,
while the set of fully raised blocks never shrinks within a stage, and this
bounds a stage by $r(\lfloor m^{2}/4\rfloor+1)$ updates.  One element of the pair
can be taken in a fixed minimum base, which bounds a stage by $1+(r-1)km$
updates (Theorem~\ref{thm:stagelength}); no bound in terms of $r$ and $k$ alone
holds (Proposition~\ref{prop:ladder}).

\paragraph{Related work.}
Besides the works above, Hertrich and Loho~\cite{hertrich-loho26} relate circuit
size to extension complexity, and Hertrich and
Stargalla~\cite{hertrich-stargalla26} study tropical circuits with scalar
multiplication gates.  The circuits of Fomin, Grigoriev and
Koshevoy~\cite{fgk16} and of Hertrich, Kober and
Loho~\cite{hertrich-kober-loho26} tropicalize generating polynomials.  Ours are
built from Boolean rank circuits, which reaches binary and all linear matroids
for bases, and weighted matroid intersection, with assignment, arborescences
and rainbow spanning trees as special cases.  On the algorithmic side,
Edmonds~\cite{edmonds70,edmonds71}, Lawler~\cite{lawler75} and
Frank~\cite{frank81} solve the same problems by comparisons and augmenting paths,
and Kasperski~\cite{kasperski08} studies possibly optimal solutions under
interval data, which we use as a filtration.

\section{Model and tools}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Circuits}

\begin{definition}[Circuits]
\label{def:circuits}
A \emph{signed tropical circuit} on inputs $x\in\R^{n}$ is a directed acyclic
graph whose sources are the coordinates of $x$ and the constant $0$, and whose
other nodes are gates $\max(a,b)$, $a+b$ and $a-b$.  A \emph{Boolean circuit}
on $x\in\{0,1\}^{n}$ has the coordinates of $x$ and the constant $0$ as sources
and gates $\wedge$, $\vee$ and $\neg$.  In both, the size is the number of gates;
sources cost nothing.  A Boolean circuit \emph{outputs an
integer in binary} if its output bits, least significant first, are the binary
digits of the integer.  A family of circuits is \emph{uniform} if a
polynomial-time algorithm produces each member from its combinatorial data,
here the ground set, the blocks and the given Boolean rank circuits.
\end{definition}

A signed tropical circuit computes a continuous piecewise linear function with
integer slopes.  The minimum $\min(a,b)=a-\max(a-b,0)$ costs three gates and
$\max(a,0)$ costs one.

\subsection{Matroids}

We use standard matroid terminology~\cite{oxley11,schrijver03}, and write
$X+e=X\cup\{e\}$, $X-e=X\setminus\{e\}$ and $[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}$.  For a matroid
$M$ on $E$ with rank function $r$, closure $\cl$ and bases $\Bases(M)$, and for a
base $B$ and $e\notin B$, the \emph{fundamental circuit} $C(B,e)$ is the unique
circuit in $B+e$.  For a weight $u\in\R^{E}$ write $u(X)=\sum_{e\in X}u_e$ and
$\beta_M(u)=\min_{B}u(B)$.  The following facts are classical.

\begin{lemma}[Minimum bases]
\label{lem:minbases}
Let $u\in\R^{E}$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A base $B$ is minimum for $u$ if and only if $u_f\le u_e$ whenever
        $e\notin B$ and $f\in C(B,e)$.
  \item A base $B$ is minimum for $u$ if and only if $B\cap\{u\le c\}$ spans
        $\{u\le c\}$ for every $c\in\R$.
  \item The minimum bases of $u$ are the bases of a matroid $M^{u}$.  For a
        minimum base $B$, $e\notin B$ and $f\in B$, the set $B-f+e$ is a minimum
        base if and only if $f\in C(B,e)$ and $u_f=u_e$, so the circuit of $e$ in
        $B+e$ in $M^{u}$ is $\{e\}\cup\{f\in C(B,e):u_f=u_e\}$.
  \item The relation ``$e=f$, or $e$ and $f$ lie on a common circuit'' is an
        equivalence relation, whose classes are the \emph{connected components};
        a loop or a coloop is a component by itself.  Closure, rank and minimum
        bases decompose over components: $e\in\cl(X)$ if and only if
        $e\in\cl(X\cap K)$ for the component $K$ of $e$, $r(X)=\sum_Kr(X\cap K)$,
        and a base $B$ is minimum if and only if every $B\cap K$ is a minimum base
        of $M|K$.  In particular the minimum bases of $u$, and $M^{u}$, depend
        only on the comparisons of $u$ within components.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Explicitly, $M^{u}$ is the direct sum, over the values $\ell$ of $u$, of the
matroids $(M|\{u\le\ell\})/\{u<\ell\}$ on the level sets $\{u=\ell\}$, so its
rank function is read from that of $M$: if $\ell_1<\cdots<\ell_q$ are the values
of $u$ and $U_i=\{u\le\ell_i\}$, $U_0=\varnothing$, then
\begin{equation}
  r_{M^{u}}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\Bigl(r\bigl(U_{i-1}\cup(A\cap\{u=\ell_i\})\bigr)-r(U_{i-1})\Bigr).
  \label{eq:rank-mu}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Threshold integrals}

The compiler of the companion paper~\cite{wang26-assign} is the bridge between
Boolean observations and signed circuits.  We restate it with explicit constants.

\begin{definition}[Threshold integral]
\label{def:ti}
For $D\in\R^{E}_{\ge0}$ and $t\ge0$ let $G_t(D)=\{e:D_e\le t\}$, and let
$0=s_0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_m$ be the entries of $D$ in increasing order with a
zero prepended.  The differences $\Delta_i=s_{i+1}-s_i$ are the \emph{gaps}.  For
$F:2^{E}\to\R$ the \emph{threshold integral} of $F$ at $D$ is
\[
  \CC_F(D)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta_i\,F\bigl(G_{s_i}(D)\bigr)
  =\int_0^{s_m}F\bigl(G_t(D)\bigr)\,dt.
\]
\end{definition}

The two expressions agree because $G_t(D)=G_{s_i}(D)$ for every $t$ in
$[s_i,s_{i+1})$.  If $F(E)=0$, the upper limit $s_m=\max_eD_e$ can be replaced
by $\infty$, since $G_t(D)=E$ for $t\ge s_m$.

\begin{theorem}[Compiler~\cite{wang26-assign}]
\label{thm:compiler}
Let one Boolean circuit of size $s$ compute $F=(F_1,\ldots,F_N):2^{E}\to\{0,1\}^{N}$
from the indicator vector of its argument.  Then a signed tropical circuit of
size at most $3ms+mN+6m^{2}$ computes $(\CC_{F_1}(D),\ldots,\CC_{F_N}(D))$ at
every $D\in\R^{E}_{\ge0}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, sorting.}  The insertion sorting network~\cite{knuth98-taocp3} has
$m(m-1)/2$ comparators, each computing
\[
  \max(a,b)\quad\text{and}\quad\min(a,b)=a+b-\max(a,b)
\]
with three gates.  It outputs $s_1,\ldots,s_m$, and one gate per $i<m$ gives
$\Delta_i=s_{i+1}-s_i$.

\emph{Step 2, membership at amplitude.}  Let $i<m$ and $e\in E$.  Since $D_e$
occurs in the sorted list, either $D_e\le s_i$ or $D_e-s_i\ge\Delta_i$, and
therefore
\begin{equation}
  \max\bigl(\Delta_i-\max(D_e-s_i,0),\,0\bigr)
  =\begin{cases}\Delta_i,&D_e\le s_i\\0,&D_e\ge s_{i+1}\end{cases}
  =\Delta_i\,\mathbf 1\bigl[e\in G_{s_i}(D)\bigr],
  \label{eq:bit}
\end{equation}
at four gates.

\emph{Step 3, lifting.}  Let $\Delta=\Delta_i\ge0$.  For $x,y\in\{0,1\}$,
\[
  \max(\Delta x+\Delta y-\Delta,0)=\Delta\,(x\wedge y),\qquad
  \max(\Delta x,\Delta y)=\Delta\,(x\vee y),\qquad
  \Delta-\Delta x=\Delta\,(\neg x).
\]
Replace each Boolean gate by the corresponding left side, at most three gates,
and each input bit by \eqref{eq:bit}.  By induction on the gates every wire
carries $\Delta$ times its Boolean value at $G_{s_i}(D)$, so the outputs are
$\Delta_iF_j(G_{s_i}(D))$, $j=1,\ldots,N$.

\emph{Step 4, accumulation and count.}  With $N$ additions per gap,
\[
  \sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta_iF_j\bigl(G_{s_i}(D)\bigr)=\CC_{F_j}(D),\qquad j=1,\ldots,N,
\]
and the size is at most
\[
  \underbrace{\tfrac32m(m-1)}_{\text{sorting}}
  +m\bigl(\underbrace{1}_{\Delta_i}+\underbrace{4m}_{\eqref{eq:bit}}
  +\underbrace{3s}_{\text{lifting}}+\underbrace{N}_{\text{sums}}\bigr)
  \le3ms+mN+6m^{2}.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Integer observables]
\label{cor:integer}
Let $f:2^{E}\to\mathbb N$, and let a Boolean circuit of size $s$ output the
binary digits of $f$ on $L$ wires.  Then a signed tropical circuit of size at
most $3ms+mL+6m^{2}+2L$ computes $\CC_f(D)$ at every $D\in\R^{E}_{\ge0}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, the digits.}  Write $f=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}2^{j}f_j$ with
$f_j:2^{E}\to\{0,1\}$ the outputs of the given circuit.  Theorem~\ref{thm:compiler}
with $N=L$ gives $c_j=\CC_{f_j}(D)$, $0\le j<L$, at $3ms+mL+6m^{2}$ gates.

\emph{Step 2, Horner's rule.}  Put $y_L=0$ and $y_j=c_j+(y_{j+1}+y_{j+1})$, two
gates per digit.  Since $F\mapsto\CC_F(D)$ is linear,
\[
  y_0=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}2^{j}c_j=\CC_{\sum_j2^{j}f_j}(D)=\CC_f(D).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Greedy without branching}
\label{sec:greedy}

The greedy algorithm reads the weights in decreasing order and keeps an element
when it raises the rank.  Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy} records the value it reaches as
a threshold integral of the rank function, with no order read at all, and the
compiler turns that integral into a circuit (Figure~\ref{fig:greedy}).

\begin{lemma}[Greedy as a threshold integral]
\label{lem:greedy}
For every $D\in\R^{E}_{\ge0}$ and $\mu\in\R$,
\begin{align*}
  h_M(D)&=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta_i\,r\bigl(E\setminus G_{s_i}(D)\bigr)=\CC_F(D)
  \quad\text{with }F(S)=r(E\setminus S),\\
  h_M(D+\mu\mathbf 1)&=h_M(D)+\mu\,r(E).
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, layer cake.}  Let $X_i=E\setminus G_{s_i}(D)=\{e:D_e>s_i\}$, and for
$e\in E$ let $s_{i_e}$ be the first occurrence of $D_e$ in the sorted list.  Then
$e\in X_i$ if and only if $i<i_e$, so
\[
  D_e=s_{i_e}-s_0=\sum_{i<i_e}\Delta_i=\sum_{i:\,e\in X_i}\Delta_i,
  \qquad
  D(B)=\sum_{e\in B}\ \sum_{i:\,e\in X_i}\Delta_i=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta_i\,|B\cap X_i|
\]
for every base $B$.

\emph{Step 2, upper bound.}  Since $B\cap X_i$ is independent and $\Delta_i\ge0$,
\[
  D(B)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta_i\,|B\cap X_i|\le\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Delta_i\,r(X_i).
\]

\emph{Step 3, a base attaining the bound.}  As $X_0\supseteq\cdots\supseteq X_{m-1}$,
there are bases $I_i$ of $X_i$ with $I_{m-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_0$; extend
$I_0$ to a base $B$.  Then $I_i\subseteq B\cap X_i$, and
\[
  r(X_i)=|I_i|\le|B\cap X_i|\le r(X_i),
\]
so equality holds in Step 2.  This is the first identity, and the second is
Definition~\ref{def:ti}.

\emph{Step 4, the shift.}  Every base has $r(E)$ elements, so
\[
  h_M(D+\mu\mathbf 1)=\max_{B}\bigl(D(B)+\mu\,|B|\bigr)=h_M(D)+\mu\,r(E).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}]
\emph{Step 1, normalization.}  Put $\mu=\min_ew_e$ and $D=w-\mu\mathbf 1\ge0$, at
$3(m-1)+m\le4m$ gates.

\emph{Step 2, the observable.}  Let $F(S)=r(E\setminus S)$.  The given circuit
on the negated input bits has size at most $s+m$, and since $F(S)\le k<2^{L}$,
its first $L$ outputs are the binary digits of $F(S)$.

\emph{Step 3, the integral.}  Corollary~\ref{cor:integer} gives $\CC_F(D)$ at
$3m(s+m)+mL+6m^{2}+2L$ gates.

\emph{Step 4, the shift.}  Doubling along the binary digits of $k$ gives $\mu k$
at $2L$ gates, and one addition gives, by Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy},
\[
  \mu k+\CC_F(D)=h_M(D)+\mu\,r(E)=h_M(D+\mu\mathbf 1)=h_M(w).
\]

\emph{Step 5, the count.}  The size is at most
\[
  4m+\bigl(3m(s+m)+mL+6m^{2}+2L\bigr)+(2L+1)=3ms+mL+9m^{2}+4m+4L+1.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
\emph{Step 1, range.}  On inputs in $\{0,1\}$ let $a_j$ be the value of the
$j$-th gate.  It is an integer, and since $|a\pm b|\le2\max(|a|,|b|)$ and
$|\max(a,b)|\le\max(|a|,|b|)$, induction gives
\[
  |a_j|\le2^{j}\le2^{S}<2^{W-1}\qquad\text{for }W=S+2.
\]
So all values are exact in $W$-bit two's complement, where sums and differences
are exact and signed comparison is the comparison of integers.

\emph{Step 2, gadgets.}  With full adders of eleven gates and $1=\neg0$, the
gadgets cost at most
\[
  a+b:\ 11W,\qquad a-b=a+\neg b+1:\ 12W+1,\qquad \max(a,b):\ 15W+10,
\]
where, with $c$ the carry out of $a+\neg b+1$,
\[
  \neg[a<b]=(a_{W-1}\oplus b_{W-1})\oplus c,\qquad
  \max(a,b)_i=\bigl([a<b]\wedge b_i\bigr)\vee\bigl(\neg[a<b]\wedge a_i\bigr).
\]

\emph{Step 3, assembly.}  Each input bit becomes a $W$-bit number with high bits
$0$, and each of the $S$ gates its gadget, at most
$S(15W+10)=15S^{2}+40S$ gates; by Step 1 the wires carry the values of the signed
circuit.

\emph{Step 4, output.}  For $T\subseteq E$,
\[
  h_M(\mathbf 1_T)=\max_{B}|B\cap T|=r(T),\qquad0\le r(T)\le2^{S}<2^{W-1},
\]
so the output bits are the binary digits of $r(T)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Equivalence]
\label{cor:equiv}
Let $\STrop(h_M)$ and $\Bool(r)$ denote the least sizes in
Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:B}.  Then
\begin{align*}
  \STrop(h_M)&\le3m\,\Bool(r)+mL+9m^{2}+4m+4L+1,\\
  \Bool(r)&\le15\,\STrop(h_M)^{2}+40\,\STrop(h_M).
\end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Linear and graphic matroids, duals]
\label{cor:classes}
For the following matroids $M$ on $m$ elements, the function $h_M$ has a signed
tropical circuit of polynomial size.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A binary matroid represented by an $r\times m$ matrix over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$:
        it has a Boolean rank circuit of size
        $m(9r^{2}+5r)+11r\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil$, so $h_M$ has a signed tropical
        circuit of size at most $100(m+1)^{2}(r+1)^{2}$.
  \item A matroid represented by an $r\times m$ matrix over a finite field with $q$
        elements: it has a Boolean rank circuit of size
        $m(6q^{2}r^{2}+3qr^{2}+r^{2}+qr+6r+1)+11r\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil+1
        \le8q^{2}(m+1)(r+1)^{2}$, so $h_M$ has a signed tropical circuit of size at
        most $25q^{2}(m+1)^{2}(r+1)^{2}$.
  \item A matroid represented over $\mathbb Q$ by an $r\times m$ integer matrix whose
        entries have absolute value less than $2^{b}$: it has a Boolean rank circuit
        of size at most $2^{19}(m+1)(r+1)^{2}(r(r+b)+1)^{6}$, so $h_M$ has a signed
        tropical circuit of size at most $2^{21}(m+1)^{2}(r+1)^{2}(r(r+b)+1)^{6}$.
  \item The dual $M^{*}$ of a matroid $M$ with a Boolean rank circuit of size $s$:
        it has a Boolean rank circuit of size
        $s+m+1+11(m+1)\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$.
  \item The cycle matroid $M(G)$ of a multigraph $G$ with $n$ vertices and $m$
        edges, whose independent sets are the edge sets containing no cycle, and its
        dual: $h_{M(G)}$ and $h_{M(G)^{*}}$ have signed tropical circuits of sizes at
        most $100(m+1)^{2}(n+1)^{2}$ and $100(m+1)^{2}((n+1)^{2}+m+1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Item 1.}  The circuit keeps $r$ slots, slot $p$ holding an occupancy bit
$f_p$ and a vector $b_p\in\mathrm{GF}(2)^{r}$, all empty at the start, and
processes column $e$ with its input bit $x_e$ by
\begin{align*}
  &v_i\leftarrow x_e\wedge A_{ie}\qquad(i\le r),\\
  &v_i\leftarrow v_i\oplus\bigl((v_p\wedge f_p)\wedge b_{p,i}\bigr)\qquad(p=1,\ldots,r,\ i\le r),\\
  &\ell_p=v_p\wedge\neg\textstyle\bigvee_{q<p}v_q,\qquad f_p\leftarrow f_p\vee\ell_p,\qquad
   b_{p,i}\leftarrow(\ell_p\wedge v_i)\vee(\neg\ell_p\wedge b_{p,i}).
\end{align*}
By induction on the columns, the occupied slots span the selected columns and
$b_p$ has its first $1$ in position $p$: after the second line $v_p=0$ at every
occupied $p$, so the third line stores the reduced column in an empty slot.
Hence the number of occupied slots is the rank, and the size is
\[
  m(9r^{2}+5r)+11r\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil,
\]
the last term for counting the occupied slots.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $k\le r$
gives the second bound.

\emph{Item 2.}  The same elimination runs over $\mathrm{GF}(q)$, an element
being carried by $q$ wires of which exactly one is true: a binary field operation
costs $2q^{2}$ gates, inversion none, masking by a bit $q$ and selection $3q$.
The column step
\[
  v\leftarrow x_eA_{\cdot e},\qquad v\leftarrow v-[f_p]\,v_p\,b_p\ \ (p=1,\ldots,r),\qquad
  f_p\leftarrow f_p\vee\ell_p,\qquad b_p\leftarrow\ell_p\,?\,v_p^{-1}v:b_p
\]
costs $6q^{2}r^{2}+3qr^{2}+r^{2}+qr+6r+1$ gates and keeps the invariant of item 1
with leading entries $1$.  Counting costs $11r\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil$ gates and
the constant $1$ one gate, and Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $k\le r$ and $q\ge2$ gives
the second bound.

\emph{Item 3.}  Put $d=2r(r+b)+1$ and $N=2^{2\lceil\log_2(d+2)\rceil+1}$, so that
$N\le8(d+1)^{2}$ and $(N+1)N^{d}<2^{N}$.  For $S\subseteq E$, with $A_S$ the
matrix of the columns in $S$, and a prime $p$,
$\operatorname{rk}_{\mathrm{GF}(p)}A_S\le\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Q}A_S$, since a
rational dependence scales to an integer one with coprime coefficients, which
stays nontrivial modulo $p$.  Conversely let $I$ be a basis of $S$ over
$\mathbb Q$ and $g=\det(A_I^{\mathsf T}A_I)$.  By Hadamard's inequality,
\[
  0<|g|\le\prod_{j\in I}\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2^{2}\le(r\,4^{b})^{r}\le2^{2r(r+b)}<2^{d}.
\]
If every prime $p\le N$ divided $g$, then $2^{\pi(N)}\le\prod_{p\le N}p\le|g|<2^{d}$,
and since $\binom Nj$ divides
$\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)=\prod_{p\le N}p^{\lfloor\log_pN\rfloor}$,
\[
  2^{N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\binom Nj\le(N+1)\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)
  \le(N+1)N^{\pi(N)}<(N+1)N^{d}<2^{N}.
\]
So some $p\le N$ does not divide $g$, $A_I$ stays independent modulo $p$, and
\[
  \operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Q}A_S=\max_{p\le N\text{ prime}}\operatorname{rk}_{\mathrm{GF}(p)}A_S.
\]
The circuits of item 2 for all primes $p\le N$ with a running maximum, by the
signed comparison of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, have at most
$2^{19}(m+1)(r+1)^{2}(r(r+b)+1)^{6}$ gates.

\emph{Item 4.}  With $W=\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$,
\[
  r^{*}(S)=|S|+r(E\setminus S)-r(E)
  \equiv\bigl(2^{W}-(r(E)\bmod2^{W})\bigr)+\sum_{e\in S}1+r(E\setminus S)\pmod{2^{W}},
\]
computed by a $W$-bit counter started at the constant and fed the $m$ input bits
($11Wm$ gates), a ripple-carry adder for $r(E\setminus S)$ ($11W$ gates), $m$
negations, the given circuit and one gate for the constant $1$.  Since
$0\le r^{*}(S)\le m<2^{W}$, the result is $r^{*}(S)$, at
$s+m+1+11(m+1)W$ gates.

\emph{Item 5.}  Let $\chi_e\in\mathrm{GF}(2)^{V(G)}$ be the column of edge $e$ in
the vertex-edge incidence matrix.  For a nonempty edge set $F$,
\[
  \sum_{e\in F}\chi_e=0\iff\deg_F(x)\equiv0\pmod2\text{ for every vertex }x,
\]
which holds for a cycle.  Conversely a nonempty $F$ with even degrees contains a
cycle: a loop, or else the last vertex of a longest path in $F$ has a second edge
in $F$, which by maximality returns to the path.  As a set of columns over
$\mathrm{GF}(2)$ is dependent exactly when a nonempty subset adds up to $0$, the
matrix represents $M(G)$, and item 1 with $r=n$ and item 4 with
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} give the two bounds.
\end{proof}

More generally, a computation of time $T$ on inputs of a fixed length is
simulated by a Boolean circuit of size $O(T\log T)$~\cite{pippenger-fischer79}.
So every matroid whose rank is computed in time polynomial in $m$ and in the
length of a fixed description has a Boolean rank circuit, and by
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} a signed tropical circuit for $h_M$, of size polynomial in
these two quantities.  Corollary~\ref{cor:transversal} gives explicit sizes for
transversal matroids and gammoids.

\begin{corollary}[Counting]
\label{cor:count}
For all large $m$, most matroids $M$ on $m$ labeled elements have no signed
tropical circuit for $h_M$ of size less than $2^{m/2}/m^{2}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, many matroids.}  Let $m\ge2$, $r=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$,
$E=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$, and let $\mathcal H$ be a largest class of $r$-subsets of
$E$ under $X\mapsto\sum_{x\in X}x\bmod m$, so
$|\mathcal H|\ge\binom mr/m$~\cite{graham-sloane80}.  Distinct $X,Y\in\mathcal H$
satisfy $|X\cap Y|\le r-2$: otherwise $X\setminus Y=\{a\}$, $Y\setminus X=\{b\}$
and
\[
  a-b\equiv\sum_{x\in X}x-\sum_{y\in Y}y\equiv0\pmod m,
\]
so $a=b$.  For $H\subseteq\mathcal H$ let $\mathcal B_H=\binom Er\setminus H$.  It
is nonempty, since for $X\in H$, $a\in X$ and $b\notin X$ we have
$|(X-a+b)\cap X|=r-1$ and so $X-a+b\notin H$.  It satisfies basis exchange: for
$B_1,B_2\in\mathcal B_H$ and $e\in B_1\setminus B_2$, either
$B_2\setminus B_1=\{f\}$ and $B_1-e+f=B_2$, or $B_2\setminus B_1\ni f_1\ne f_2$ and
\[
  \bigl|(B_1-e+f_1)\cap(B_1-e+f_2)\bigr|=r-1,
\]
so $B_1-e+f_1$ or $B_1-e+f_2$ lies in $\mathcal B_H$.  Distinct $H$ give distinct
matroids, and $\binom mr\ge2^{m}/(m+1)$ as the largest of $m+1$ coefficients with
sum $2^{m}$, so the number of matroids on $E$ is at least
\[
  2^{|\mathcal H|}\ge2^{\binom mr/m}\ge2^{2^{m}/(m(m+1))}.
\]
Knuth~\cite{knuth74} proves the stronger bound $2^{2^{m-\frac32\log_2m-O(1)}}$.

\emph{Step 2, few small circuits.}  Boolean circuits of size $s'$ on $m$ inputs
with at most $m$ outputs number $2^{O(s'\log(s'+m))}$, and each computes at most
one rank function.  If $h_M$ has a signed tropical circuit of size
$S<2^{m/2}/m^{2}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, keeping the first $m$ output bits, gives a
Boolean rank circuit of size
\[
  s'\le15S^{2}+40S<\frac{15\cdot2^{m}}{m^{4}}+\frac{40\cdot2^{m/2}}{m^{2}}<\frac{2^{m}}{m^{7/2}}
\]
for large $m$, and
\[
  2^{O(s'\log(s'+m))}=2^{O(2^{m}m^{-5/2})}=o\bigl(2^{2^{m}/(m(m+1))}\bigr).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Setting and the Lagrangian}
\label{sec:lagr}

Throughout this section $M$ is a matroid of rank $k$ on $E$, $|E|=m$, the blocks
$R_1,\ldots,R_k$ partition $E$, $\blk(e)$ is the block of $e$, and
$E_Q=\bigcup_{b\in Q}R_b$ for $Q\subseteq[k]$.  A set is \emph{rainbow} if it
meets every block at most once.  For a matroid $N$ on $E$ let $\nu(N)$ be the
largest size of a rainbow independent set of $N$, and let $\Av(N)$ be the set of
blocks missed by some rainbow independent set of size $\nu(N)$; we call these
blocks \emph{avoidable}.  These notions, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:avoidable}
and~\ref{lem:augment} below, apply with the same proofs to any partition of a
finite ground set into any number of blocks, and Section~\ref{sec:two} uses them
in that generality.  We assume that $M$ has a rainbow base, which the generator
of the circuit decides once by unweighted matroid intersection.

\begin{proposition}[Rainbow spanning trees contain assignment]
\label{prop:star}
For every bipartite graph $H=(L,R,E)$ with $|L|=|R|=n$ there is a multigraph with
colored edges whose rainbow spanning trees are the perfect matchings of $H$, with
the same weights.  Its rainbow spanning tree polynomial is the permanent of the
biadjacency matrix of $H$ with variable entries.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, construction.}  On the vertex set $\{z\}\cup R$ take, for each edge
$ij\in E$ with $i\in L$ and $j\in R$, an edge $zj$ of color $i$ and weight
$w_{ij}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:star}).

\emph{Step 2, correspondence.}  Each $j\in R$ is adjacent only to $z$, so the
spanning trees are the sets $T_\pi$ containing, for each $j\in R$, the edge $zj$ of
color $\pi(j)$, where $\pi:R\to L$ and $\pi(j)j\in E$.  The tree $T_\pi$ is rainbow
if and only if $\pi$ is injective, that is, $\{\pi(j)j:j\in R\}$ is a perfect
matching of $H$, of weight $w(T_\pi)$.  With variable weights $x_{ij}$,
\[
  \sum_{T_\pi\text{ rainbow}}\ \prod_{j\in R}x_{\pi(j)j}
  =\sum_{\pi:R\to L\text{ bijective}}\ \prod_{j\in R}x_{\pi(j)j}=\operatorname{per}(X),
\]
where $X$ is the biadjacency matrix with entries $x_{ij}$ on the edges and $0$
elsewhere.
\end{proof}

For $p\in\R^{k}$ write $v=w-p_{\blk}$, that is $v_e=w_e-p_{\blk(e)}$, and
\[
  \Psi(p)=\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b+\beta_M(v),\qquad \nu_0=\nu(M^{v}),\qquad
  S_0=\Av(M^{v}).
\]
Since rainbow independent sets of $M^{v}$ extend to minimum bases of $v$, and a
minimum base meeting $j$ blocks contains a rainbow set of size $j$, $\nu_0$ is the
largest number of blocks met by a minimum base of $v$, and $S_0$ is the set of
blocks missed by some minimum base meeting $\nu_0$ blocks.

\begin{lemma}[Weak duality and certificate]
\label{lem:duality}
$\Psi(p)\le\Opt(w)$ for every $p$, and $\Psi(p)=\Opt(w)$ whenever $\nu_0=k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, weak duality.}  A rainbow base $B$ meets every block once, so
\[
  w(B)=\sum_{e\in B}\bigl(v_e+p_{\blk(e)}\bigr)=\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b+v(B)
  \ge\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b+\beta_M(v)=\Psi(p).
\]

\emph{Step 2, the certificate.}  If $\nu_0=k$, a rainbow independent set of
$M^{v}$ of size $k$ is a rainbow base $B$ with $v(B)=\beta_M(v)$, and
\[
  \Opt(w)\le w(B)=\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b+\beta_M(v)=\Psi(p)\le\Opt(w).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Possibly optimal bases}
\label{sec:pos}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:theta}
For a base $B$ let
$\theta(B)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_f-v_e:e\notin B,\ f\in C(B,e)\}\bigr)$, the
largest gain of a single exchange, and let
$\Pos_t=\{B\in\Bases(M):\theta(B)\le t\}$ for $t\ge0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Characterizations]
\label{prop:pos}
For a base $B$ and $t\ge0$ the following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\theta(B)\le t$.
  \item $B$ is a minimum base for $v-t\mathbf 1_B$.
  \item $v(B)-v(B')\le t\,|B\setminus B'|$ for every base $B'$.
  \item $e\in\cl\bigl(B\cap\{g:v_g\le v_e+t\}\bigr)$ for every $e\in E$.
  \item $B$ is a minimum base for some $u$ with $\|u-v\|_\infty\le t/2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, $e\notin B$ and $f\in C(B,e)-e\subseteq B$, and $u=v-t\mathbf 1_B$.

\emph{(1)$\Leftrightarrow$(2).}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1), (2) says
$u_f\le u_e$ for all such $e,f$, and $u_f-u_e=v_f-v_e-t$.

\emph{(2)$\Rightarrow$(3).}  For a base $B'$, $u(B)\le u(B')$ gives
\[
  v(B)-v(B')\le t\bigl(|B|-|B\cap B'|\bigr)=t\,|B\setminus B'|.
\]

\emph{(3)$\Rightarrow$(1).}  For the base $B'=B-f+e$,
$v_f-v_e=v(B)-v(B')\le t\,|B\setminus B'|=t$.

\emph{(1)$\Leftrightarrow$(4).}  For $e\in B$, (4) holds trivially, and for
$e\notin B$,
\[
  e\in\cl\bigl(B\cap\{g:v_g\le v_e+t\}\bigr)\iff C(B,e)-e\subseteq\{g:v_g\le v_e+t\}.
\]

\emph{(2)$\Rightarrow$(5).}  The weight $u+\tfrac t2\mathbf 1$ has the minimum
bases of $u$, and
$\|u+\tfrac t2\mathbf 1-v\|_\infty=\|\tfrac t2\mathbf 1-t\mathbf 1_B\|_\infty=\tfrac t2$.

\emph{(5)$\Rightarrow$(1).}  If $B$ is minimum for $u'$ with
$\|u'-v\|_\infty\le t/2$, then $u'_f\le u'_e$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1), and
\[
  v_f-v_e\le\bigl(u'_f+\tfrac t2\bigr)-\bigl(u'_e-\tfrac t2\bigr)\le t.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

So $\Pos_t$ is the family of bases that are possibly optimal for the interval
weights $[v-\tfrac t2,v+\tfrac t2]$~\cite{kasperski08}.  It grows with $t$,
equals $\Bases(M^{v})$ at $t=0$, and equals $\Bases(M)$ for
$t\ge\max v-\min v$.

\begin{proposition}[Assignment]
\label{prop:assign-pos}
If $M$ is the partition matroid of the columns of a bipartite graph and the blocks
are its rows, then $\theta(B)=\max_{f\in B}D_f$ with
$D_{ij}=v_{ij}-\min_{i'}v_{i'j}$, so $\Pos_t$ is the set of bases inside the
threshold graph $\{D\le t\}$ of the companion paper~\cite{wang26-assign}, read
along the trajectory in which the column potentials equal the column minima.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $e\notin B$ in column $j$, $C(B,e)=\{e,f\}$ with $f$ the edge of $B$ in
column $j$, so
\[
  \theta(B)=\max_{f\in B}\ \max\Bigl(\{0\}\cup\bigl\{v_f-v_e:e\ne f\text{ in column }j\bigr\}\Bigr)
  =\max_{f\in B}\Bigl(v_f-\min_{i'}v_{i'j}\Bigr)=\max_{f\in B}D_f.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Block shifts stay possibly optimal]
\label{lem:path}
If $y\in[0,s]^{k}$, every minimum base of $v-y_{\blk}$ lies in $\Pos_s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $B$ be a minimum base of $v-y_{\blk}$, $e\notin B$ and $f\in C(B,e)$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1), $v_f-y_{\blk(f)}\le v_e-y_{\blk(e)}$, so
\[
  v_f-v_e\le y_{\blk(f)}-y_{\blk(e)}\le s,
\]
and $\theta(B)\le s$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two obstacles}
\label{sec:obstacles}

The companion paper reads the matching structure of its threshold graphs, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:assign-pos} identifies them with $\Pos_t$.  For a general
matroid this family cannot play the same role.

\begin{proposition}[Not a matroid]
\label{prop:notmatroid}
For $U_{2,4}$ on $\{a,b,c,d\}$ with $v=(0,1,2,3)$, $\Pos_2=\{ab,ac,ad,bc\}$,
which violates the basis exchange axiom.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, the values of $\theta$.}  In $U_{2,4}$, $C(B,e)=B+e$ for
$e\notin B$, so
\[
  \theta(B)=\max\Bigl(0,\ \max_{f\in B}v_f-\min_{e\notin B}v_e\Bigr),\qquad
  \bigl(\theta(ab),\theta(ac),\theta(ad),\theta(bc),\theta(bd),\theta(cd)\bigr)=(0,1,2,2,3,3).
\]

\emph{Step 2, the exchange fails.}  For $B_1=ad$, $B_2=bc$ and
$a\in B_1\setminus B_2$, both candidates $B_1-a+f$, $f\in B_2\setminus B_1$, have
$\theta(bd)=\theta(cd)=3>2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Querying $\Pos_t$ is NP-complete]
\label{prop:nphard}
Given a multigraph with colored edges, edge weights $v$ and $t\ge0$, deciding
whether $\Pos_t$ contains a rainbow spanning tree is NP-complete.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, membership in NP.}  For a rainbow spanning tree $T$,
$\theta(T)=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{v_g-v_e:e\notin T,\ g\in C(T,e)\}\bigr)$ is
computed from the fundamental cycles in polynomial time.

\emph{Step 2, the construction.}  We reduce from rainbow path, NP-complete by
\cite{cfmy09}: given a graph $H$ with colored edges and vertices $a,b$, is there a
path from $a$ to $b$ with distinct colors?  Let $C$ be the colors of $H$,
$q=|C|$, and $h\ge q$ the number of vertices of $H$ (add isolated vertices if
needed).  On $V(H)\cup\{z,y\}$ take the edges of $H$ with weight $5$, an edge
$e^{*}=ab$ of weight $0$ and a new color $c^{*}$, an edge $f=ya$ of weight $0$ and
color $c^{*}$, and for every $x\in V(H)$ and every color in $C\cup X$ an edge $zx$
of that color and weight $10$, where $X$ is a set of $h-q$ new colors
(Figure~\ref{fig:gadget}).  There are $h+2$ vertices and $h+1$ colors, so a
rainbow spanning tree uses every color once.  Take $t=5$.

\emph{Step 3, from a tree to a path.}  Let $T$ be a rainbow spanning tree with
$\theta(T)\le5$.  As $f$ is the only edge at $y$ and has the color of $e^{*}$,
$f\in T$ and $e^{*}\notin T$.  Every edge $g$ of the path $P_T$ of $T$ from $a$
to $b$ lies in $C(T,e^{*})$, so
\[
  v_g=v_g-v_{e^{*}}\le\theta(T)\le5<10.
\]
So $P_T$ avoids $z$ and the leaf $y$, lies in $H$, and is rainbow since $T$ is.

\emph{Step 4, from a path to a tree.}  Let $P$ be a rainbow path from $a$ to $b$
in $H$.  Let $T$ consist of $E(P)$, $f$, an edge $zx$ for each
$x\in V(H)\setminus V(P)$ and one edge $zx$ with $x\in V(P)$, these
$h-|E(P)|$ edges at $z$ taking the $h-|E(P)|$ colors of $C\cup X$ not on $P$.
Then $T$ is a rainbow spanning tree with $C(T,e^{*})=P+e^{*}$ and edge weights at
most $10$, so
\[
  \theta(T)\le\max\bigl(\underbrace{5-0}_{e=e^{*}},\ \underbrace{10-5}_{e\in E(H)\setminus T},\
  \underbrace{10-10}_{e=zx\notin T}\bigr)=5.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Matroidal enlargements}
\label{sec:enlarge}

The way around both obstacles is to keep the containment of $\Pos_t$ and drop
its exactness.

\begin{definition}[Admissible family]
\label{def:admissible}
Let $\nu_0=r<k$.  A family of matroids $N(t)$ on $E$, $t\ge0$, is
\emph{admissible} if
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(F1)] $N(0)=M^{v}$;
  \item[(F2)] every base of $N(t)$ is a base of $N(t')$ whenever $t\le t'$;
  \item[(F3)] $\Pos_t\subseteq\Bases(N(t))$ for every $t$;
  \item[(F4)] $N(t)$ has a rainbow base for all large $t$;
  \item[(F5)] there are levels $0=c_0<c_1<\cdots<c_q$ such that $N(t)$ is
        constant on each $[c_i,c_{i+1})$ and on $[c_q,\infty)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The update]
\label{def:update}
For an admissible family let $\nu(t)=\nu(N(t))$,
\[
  \tau=\min\{t:\nu(t)\ge r+1\},\qquad
  \lambda_b=\min\{t:\text{some base of }N(t)\text{ meeting exactly }r\text{ blocks misses }b\},
\]
with $\lambda_b=\infty$ if there is none, and $a_b=(\tau-\lambda_b)_+$.  The
update is $p\mapsto p+a$.  If $\nu_0>r$ we set $a=0$.
\end{definition}

The minima exist: by (F2) independent sets of $N(t)$ stay independent in $N(t')$
for $t\le t'$, so
\[
  r=\nu(0)\le\nu(t)\le\nu(t')\qquad(t\le t'),
\]
by (F1); $\nu(t)=k>r$ for large $t$ by (F4); and by (F5) both sets of levels are
unions of intervals $[c_i,c_{i+1})$ and $[c_q,\infty)$.  For $t<\tau$ we have
$\nu(t)=r$, a base of $N(t)$ meeting exactly $r$ blocks contains a rainbow
independent set of size $r$ missing the same blocks, and a rainbow independent
set of size $r$ extends to a base of $N(t)$ meeting no further block.  With (F2)
this gives $b\in\Av(N(t))\iff\lambda_b\le t$ for $t<\tau$, and hence
\begin{equation}
  a_b=(\tau-\lambda_b)_+
  =\int_0^{\infty}\mathbf 1\bigl[\nu(N(t))\le r\bigr]\;\mathbf 1\bigl[b\in\Av(N(t))\bigr]\,dt.
  \label{eq:update-integral}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:axis} draws the integrand.

\begin{lemma}[Avoidable blocks]
\label{lem:avoidable}
Let $N$ be a matroid on $E$ and $\nu=\nu(N)$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $|Q|-r_N(E_Q)\le k-\nu$ for every $Q\subseteq[k]$.
  \item $|\Av(N)|-r_N(E_{\Av(N)})=k-\nu$.
  \item $\Av(N)\subseteq Q$ for every $Q$ attaining equality in item 1.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, item 1.}  A rainbow independent set $J$ with $|J|=\nu$ satisfies
\[
  \nu=|J\cap E_Q|+|J\setminus E_Q|\le r_N(E_Q)+(k-|Q|).
\]

\emph{Step 2, the exchange graph.}  Fix a rainbow independent set $I$ with
$|I|=\nu$, and let $D_I$ be the directed graph on $E$ with arcs
\[
  y\to x\ \ \text{if }I-x+y\text{ is independent},\qquad
  x\to y\ \ \text{if }\blk(y)=\blk(x)\qquad(x\in I,\ y\notin I),
\]
sources the $y\notin I$ in blocks missed by $I$, and sinks the $y\notin I$ with
$I+y$ independent.  As $I$ is maximum, no path leads from a source to a
sink~\cite{lawler75,schrijver03}.  Let $R$ be the set reachable from the sources
and $A'=\{b:b\text{ missed by }I\}\cup\blk(I\cap R)$, so
$|A'|=(k-\nu)+|I\cap R|$.

\emph{Step 3, $A'\subseteq\Av(N)$.}  Blocks missed by $I$ are avoidable.  For
$x\in I\cap R$ and a shortest path $y_0\to x_1\to y_1\to\cdots\to y_{j-1}\to x_j=x$
from a source $y_0$, the set
\[
  I-\{x_1,\ldots,x_j\}+\{y_0,\ldots,y_{j-1}\}
\]
is rainbow, has $\nu$ elements, misses $\blk(x)$, and is independent by the
exchange lemma for shortest paths~\cite{schrijver03}.

\emph{Step 4, $r_N(E_{A'})\le|I\cap R|$.}  Let $y\in E_{A'}\setminus I$.  Either
$y$ is a source or $x\to y$ for some $x\in I\cap R$, so $y\in R$ is not a sink
and $y\in\cl_N(I)$.  Each $x'\ne y$ in the circuit of $y$ in $I+y$ gives an arc
$y\to x'$, so $x'\in R$.  Since also $I\cap E_{A'}\subseteq I\cap R$,
\[
  E_{A'}\subseteq\cl_N(I\cap R),\qquad r_N(E_{A'})\le|I\cap R|.
\]

\emph{Step 5, item 3.}  Let $Q$ attain equality in item 1, and let
$b\in\Av(N)\setminus Q$ be missed by a rainbow independent $J$ with $|J|=\nu$.
Then
\[
  |J\setminus E_Q|=\nu-|J\cap E_Q|\ge\nu-r_N(E_Q)=\nu-|Q|+(k-\nu)=k-|Q|,
\]
so $J$ meets all $k-|Q|$ blocks outside $Q$, $b$ among them, a contradiction.

\emph{Step 6, item 2.}  By item 1 and Steps 2 and 4,
\[
  k-\nu\ge|A'|-r_N(E_{A'})\ge(k-\nu)+|I\cap R|-|I\cap R|=k-\nu,
\]
so $A'$ attains equality, $\Av(N)\subseteq A'$ by Step 5, and $A'=\Av(N)$ by
Step 3.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Validity]
\label{thm:validity}
Let $\nu_0=r<k$, let $N(t)$ be admissible, and let $a$ be its update.  Then
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Psi(p+a)=\Psi(p)+(k-r)\tau$;
  \item every base of $M^{v}$ meeting $r$ blocks is a minimum base of
        $v-a_{\blk}$; in particular $\nu_0$ does not decrease.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, the path.}  For $s\in[0,\tau]$ put $a(s)_b=(s-\lambda_b)_+\in[0,s]$,
$v(s)=v-a(s)_{\blk}$ and $\varphi(s)=\Psi(p+a(s))$, so $a(0)=0$ and $a(\tau)=a$.
As a minimum of finitely many continuous piecewise linear functions of $s$,
$\varphi$ is continuous and piecewise linear, and
\[
  \varphi(s)-\varphi(0)=\int_0^{s}\varphi'(\sigma^{+})\,d\sigma.
\]

\emph{Step 2, the right derivative.}  Let $S_s=\{b:\lambda_b\le s\}$.  For small
$h>0$, $a(s+h)=a(s)+h\mathbf 1_{S_s}$, and the minimum bases of
$v(s)-h\mathbf 1_{E_{S_s}}$ are the minimum bases of $v(s)$ with the most elements
in $E_{S_s}$.  Hence
\[
  \varphi'(s^{+})=|S_s|-\max\bigl\{|B\cap E_{S_s}|:B\in\Bases(M^{v(s)})\bigr\}.
\]

\emph{Step 3, the sandwich.}  Let $s<\tau$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:path} with
$y=a(s)$ and by (F3),
\[
  \Bases(M^{v(s)})\subseteq\Pos_s\subseteq\Bases(N(s)),
\]
and $\nu(N(s))=r$, $S_s=\Av(N(s))$ by the paragraph after
Definition~\ref{def:update}.  So Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2) gives, for every
$B\in\Bases(M^{v(s)})$,
\[
  |B\cap E_{S_s}|\le r_{N(s)}(E_{S_s})=|S_s|-(k-r),
\]
hence $\varphi'(s^{+})\ge k-r$, and by Step 1, $\varphi(s)\ge\Psi(p)+(k-r)s$ for
$s\in[0,\tau]$.

\emph{Step 4, an old base.}  Let $B^{*}$ be a base of $M^{v}$ meeting exactly $r$
blocks.  Its $k-r$ missed blocks lie in $S_0\subseteq S_s$, as $\lambda_b=0$ on
$S_0$, and $B^{*}\in\Bases(N(s))$ by (F1) and (F2), so for $s<\tau$
\[
  |S_s|-(k-r)\le|B^{*}\cap E_{S_s}|\le r_{N(s)}(E_{S_s})=|S_s|-(k-r).
\]
Hence $v(s)(B^{*})+\sum_ba(s)_b$ has right derivative
$-(|S_s|-(k-r))+|S_s|=k-r$, so
$v(s)(B^{*})=\beta_M(v)-\sum_ba(s)_b+(k-r)s$, and
\[
  \varphi(s)\le\sum_{b=1}^{k}\bigl(p_b+a(s)_b\bigr)+v(s)(B^{*})
  =\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b+\beta_M(v)+(k-r)s=\Psi(p)+(k-r)s.
\]

\emph{Step 5, conclusion.}  By Steps 3 and 4, for $s\in[0,\tau]$,
\[
  \varphi(s)=\Psi(p)+(k-r)s,\qquad v(s)(B^{*})=\beta_M(v(s)).
\]
At $s=\tau$ these are items 1 and 2, and $\nu_0$ does not decrease since $B^{*}$
meets $r$ blocks.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Single linkage on possibly optimal elements}
\label{sec:posl}

\begin{definition}[PO-SL]
\label{def:posl}
For $t\ge0$ let
\[
  \PO_t=\bigl\{f\in E:f\notin\cl\{g:v_g<v_f-t\}\bigr\}.
\]
In each connected component $K$ of $M$, link $x,y\in\PO_t\cap K$ when
$|v_x-v_y|\le t$, and call the classes of the transitive closure of this relation
the \emph{clusters} of $K$ at scale $t$.  Each cluster is an interval of the
sorted values of $\PO_t\cap K$ in which consecutive values differ by at most $t$,
and consecutive values in different clusters differ by more than $t$.  So the
clusters of $K$ are ordered: every value of a cluster lies more than $t$ below
every value of the next one.  For $e\in\PO_t$ let $u_t(e)$ be the position of the
cluster of $e$ in this order, $1$ for the lowest cluster of its component, let
$u_t(e)=m+1$ for $e\notin\PO_t$, and let $N(t)=M^{u_t}$.
\end{definition}

Values of $u_t$ in different components are never compared, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(4) they need not be.  The inequalities matter: $\PO_t$
uses a strict inequality and the linkage a non-strict one
(Figure~\ref{fig:posl}).  With $v_g\le v_f-t$ in $\PO_t$, property (F1) below
fails already for $U_{1,3}$ and $v=(0,0,1)$, and with $|v_x-v_y|<t$ in the
linkage, the two elements of value $0$ there form two clusters at scale $0$ that
are not ordered.  Write $G_t=\{(e,f)\in E\times E:v_f-v_e\le t\}$.

\begin{proposition}[PO-SL is admissible]
\label{prop:posl}
For every $v$, the family $N(t)$ of Definition~\ref{def:posl}
satisfies (F1)--(F5) with levels $\{0\}\cup\{|v_e-v_f|:e,f\in E\}$.  It is
determined by $G_t$.  For the partition matroid of the columns of a bipartite
graph, $N(t)$ is the column matroid restricted to the threshold graph
$\{D\le t\}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:assign-pos}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, (F1).}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(2), $\PO_0$ is the set of
elements of minimum bases of $v$, and at scale $0$ clusters are classes of equal
values, so within a component
\[
  u_0(x)\le u_0(y)\iff v_x\le v_y\qquad(x,y\in\PO_0).
\]
If $B$ is minimum for $v$, then $B\subseteq\PO_0$, and for $e\notin B$ and
$f\in C(B,e)$ either $u_0(e)=m+1$ or $v_f\le v_e$ gives $u_0(f)\le u_0(e)$; so
$B$ is minimum for $u_0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1).  Conversely let $B$ be
minimum for $u_0$, $c\in\R$, and $K_x$ the component of $x$.  For
$x\in\PO_0\setminus B$ with $v_x\le c$, Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1) gives
\[
  C(B,x)-x\subseteq\{f\in\PO_0\cap K_x:u_0(f)\le u_0(x)\}\subseteq\{v\le c\},
\]
and $x\notin\PO_0$ lies in $\cl\{g:v_g<v_x\}$.  By induction on $v_x$,
$\{v\le c\}\subseteq\cl(B\cap\{v\le c\})$ for every $c$, so $B$ is minimum for $v$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(2).

\emph{Step 2, (F3).}  Let $\theta(B)\le t$, so that $B$ is minimum for
$u=v-t\mathbf 1_B$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:pos}.  For $f\in B$ and
$v_g<v_f-t$,
\[
  u_g\le v_g<v_f-t=u_f,
\]
and an element of a minimum base is not spanned by strictly cheaper ones, so
$f\in\PO_t$.  For $e\notin B$ and $f\in C(B,e)-e$,
\[
  u_t(f)\le u_t(e)\quad\text{since}\quad
  \begin{cases}
    u_t(e)=m+1,&e\notin\PO_t,\\
    \text{clusters are ordered by value},&e\in\PO_t,\ v_f\le v_e,\\
    0<v_f-v_e\le\theta(B)\le t\text{ links }e\text{ and }f,&e\in\PO_t,\ v_f>v_e,
  \end{cases}
\]
and $B$ is minimum for $u_t$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1).

\emph{Step 3, (F2).}  A minimum base $B$ of $u_t$ lies in $\PO_t$: as
$\PO_t\supseteq\PO_0$ spans $E$, Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(2) at level $m$ gives
\[
  E\subseteq\cl(\PO_t)=\cl\bigl(B\cap\{u_t\le m\}\bigr)=\cl(B\cap\PO_t),
\]
so $B\cap\PO_t=B$.  Let $t\le t'$, $B$ minimum for $u_t$, $e\notin B$ and
$f\in C(B,e)-e$, in a component $K$.  As $\PO_t\subseteq\PO_{t'}$ and linkage is
monotone, each cluster at scale $t$ lies in one at scale $t'$, in the same order.
We show $u_{t'}(f)\le u_{t'}(e)$, so that $B$ is minimum for $u_{t'}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1).
\begin{itemize}
  \item $e\notin\PO_{t'}$: $u_{t'}(e)=m+1$.
  \item $e\in\PO_t$: $u_t(f)\le u_t(e)$, and this persists at $t'$.
  \item $e\in\PO_{t'}\setminus\PO_t$, $v_f\le v_e$: the order at scale $t'$.
  \item $e\in\PO_{t'}\setminus\PO_t$, $v_f>v_e$.  Every $g\in K$ lies in
        $\cl\{h\in B\cap K:u_t(h)\le u_t(x_g)\}$ for some $x_g\in\PO_t\cap K$ with
        $v_{x_g}\le v_g$: take $x_g=g$ if $g\in\PO_t$, by
        Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1), and if $g\notin\PO_t$ use
        $g\in\cl\{h\in K:v_h<v_g-t\}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(4)) and induction
        on $v_g$.  Since $e\notin\PO_t$, the same argument gives $x^{*}\in\PO_t\cap K$
        with
        \[
          e\in\cl\bigl\{h\in B\cap K:u_t(h)\le u_t(x^{*})\bigr\},\qquad v_{x^{*}}<v_e-t,
        \]
        so $u_t(f)\le u_t(x^{*})$.  As $v_{x^{*}}<v_e<v_f$, the elements $f$ and
        $x^{*}$ share a cluster at scale $t$, hence at scale $t'$, and this cluster,
        an interval of the sorted values of $\PO_{t'}\cap K$, contains $e$.  So
        $u_{t'}(f)=u_{t'}(e)$.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Step 4, (F4) and (F5).}  For $t\ge\max v-\min v$, $\{g:v_g<v_f-t\}=\varnothing$
for all $f$, so $\PO_t$ is the set of non-loops, the non-loops of each component
form one cluster, every base is minimum for $u_t$, and $N(t)=M$.  The sets
$\{g:v_g<v_f-t\}$ and the linkage are constant on each half-open interval between
consecutive levels of $\{0\}\cup\{|v_e-v_f|:e,f\in E\}$ and beyond the last one.

\emph{Step 5, determined by $G_t$.}  We have
\[
  v_g<v_f-t\iff(g,f)\notin G_t,\qquad |v_x-v_y|\le t\iff(x,y),(y,x)\in G_t,
\]
and for $x,y$ in different clusters of a component $v_x<v_y\iff(x,y)\notin G_t$;
the components depend on $M$ alone.

\emph{Step 6, assignment.}  In the partition matroid of the columns, each column
is a component or a coloop, in which every nonempty set spans the column.  So
for $f$ in column $j$,
\[
  f\in\PO_t\iff v_f-\min_{i'}v_{i'j}\le t\iff D_f\le t,
\]
and these edges lie within $t$ of the column minimum, in one cluster.  Hence
$N(t)$ is the column matroid restricted to $\{D\le t\}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Termination}
\label{sec:term}

Fix a stage $r<k$ and a state with $\nu_0=r$, let $a$ be its update under PO-SL,
and let $v'=v-a_{\blk}$.  The blocks of $S_0=\Av(M^{v})$ are raised by the full
length $\tau$ of the update, and the others by less.  Let
\[
  \Pi(v)=\bigl\{(y,g):y\in E_{S_0},\ g\notin E_{S_0},\ v_y\le v_g\bigr\},
\]
the pairs in which an element of a fully raised block lies at or below an
element of another block, ties included.  The ties matter: with strict
inequality, item 3 below fails on the cycle matroid of $K_4$.

\begin{theorem}[Potential]
\label{thm:potential}
The following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $\nu(M^{v'})=r$, then $\Av(M^{v'})\supseteq S_0$.
  \item $\Pi(v')\supseteq\Pi(v)$, both formed with $S_0$.
  \item $\Pi(v')\ne\Pi(v)$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently stage $r$ ends after at most $r\bigl(\lfloor m^{2}/4\rfloor+1\bigr)$
updates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, item 1.}  For $b\in S_0$, some base of $M^{v}$ meeting exactly $r$
blocks misses $b$; by Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}(2) it is a base of $M^{v'}$, so
$b\in\Av(M^{v'})$ when $\nu(M^{v'})=r$.

\emph{Step 2, item 2.}  We have $a_b=\tau$ for $b\in S_0$, where $\lambda_b=0$,
and $a_b=(\tau-\lambda_b)_+\le\tau$ otherwise, so for $y\in E_{S_0}$ and
$g\notin E_{S_0}$
\[
  v'_y-v'_g=(v_y-\tau)-\bigl(v_g-a_{\blk(g)}\bigr)\le v_y-v_g.
\]

\emph{Step 3, the first event.}  Let
$\delta=\min\{t:\Av(N(t))\ne S_0\text{ or }\nu(N(t))>r\}$, which exists by (F4)
and (F5).  Since $N(0)=M^{v}$ and $\nu(N(\tau))\ge r+1$, $0<\delta\le\tau$, and
since $\Av(N(t))=S_0$ for $t<\delta$, every $b\notin S_0$ has
\[
  \lambda_b\ge\delta,\qquad a_b\le\tau-\delta.
\]

\emph{Step 4, a rank jump.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2) for $M^{v}$,
$r_{M^{v}}(E_{S_0})=|S_0|-(k-r)$, and we claim
\begin{equation}
  r_{N(\delta)}(E_{S_0})>|S_0|-(k-r).
  \label{eq:jump}
\end{equation}
If $\nu(N(\delta))>r$, Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(1) gives
$|S_0|-r_{N(\delta)}(E_{S_0})\le k-\nu(N(\delta))<k-r$.  If $\nu(N(\delta))=r$, then
$S_0\subsetneq\Av(N(\delta))$ by (F1), (F2) and the choice of $\delta$, so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(3) the set $S_0$ does not attain equality in
Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(1).

\emph{Step 5, a tie across $S_0$.}  Let $B$ be a base of $M^{v}$ with
$|B\cap E_{S_0}|=r_{M^{v}}(E_{S_0})$.  By (F1), (F2) and \eqref{eq:jump},
$B\in\Bases(N(\delta))$ and $B\cap E_{S_0}$ does not span $E_{S_0}$ in $N(\delta)$,
so some $y\in E_{S_0}\setminus B$ has, in $N(\delta)$, a circuit in $B+y$ through
some $g\in B\setminus E_{S_0}$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(3) for $u_\delta$ and
$B\subseteq\PO_\delta$ (Step 3 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:posl}),
\[
  g\in C(B,y),\qquad u_\delta(g)=u_\delta(y)\le m,
\]
so $g$ and $y$ lie in one cluster at scale $\delta$.  Minimality of $B$ for $v$
gives $v_g\le v_y$, and $v_g=v_y$ would make $B-g+y$ a minimum base of $v$ with
more elements in $E_{S_0}$, so $v_g<v_y$.

\emph{Step 6, a crossing pair.}  In this cluster let
\[
  \alpha=\max\{v_z:z\notin E_{S_0},\ v_z<v_y\}=v_{z_1},\qquad
  \beta=\min\{v_z:z\in E_{S_0},\ v_z>\alpha\}=v_{z_2},
\]
the sets being nonempty by $g$ and $y$, so $\beta\le v_y$.  By the choice of
$\alpha$ and $\beta$ no value of the cluster lies in $(\alpha,\beta)$, so they are
consecutive and $0<\beta-\alpha\le\delta$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cross}).

\emph{Step 7, item 3.}  By Steps 2 and 3,
\[
  v'_{z_2}-v'_{z_1}\le(\beta-\tau)-\bigl(\alpha-(\tau-\delta)\bigr)=(\beta-\alpha)-\delta\le0,
\]
so $(z_2,z_1)\in\Pi(v')\setminus\Pi(v)$, as $\beta>\alpha$.

\emph{Step 8, counting.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2) for $M^{v}$ and
$r_{M^{v}}(E)=k$,
\[
  |S_0|-r_{M^{v}}(E_{S_0})=k-r\ge1,\qquad\text{so}\qquad k-r\le|S_0|\le k-1.
\]
By item 1, $S_0$ only grows within a stage, so it takes at most $r$ values, and
while it is fixed, items 2 and 3 make $\Pi$ grow strictly at every update, with
\[
  |\Pi|\le|E_{S_0}|\,|E\setminus E_{S_0}|\le\lfloor m^{2}/4\rfloor.
\]
So a stage has at most $r(\lfloor m^{2}/4\rfloor+1)$ updates.
\end{proof}

The pairs of Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} range over all of
$E_{S_0}\times(E\setminus E_{S_0})$.  A fixed minimum base confines them: at every
update some pair with an element in the base crosses, and there are at most $km$
such pairs.

\begin{theorem}[Stage length]
\label{thm:stagelength}
Let $B$ be a base of $M^{v}$ meeting exactly $r$ blocks.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Some $x\in E_{S_0}$ and $z\notin E_{S_0}$ in one connected component of $M$,
        one of them in $B$, satisfy $v_x>v_z$ and $v'_x\le v'_z$.
  \item If $|S_0|=k-r$ and $\nu(M^{v'})=r$, then $\Av(M^{v'})\ne S_0$.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently stage $r$ ends after at most $1+(r-1)km$ updates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use $\delta$ of Step 3 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} and write
$\rho=|S_0|-(k-r)$.

\emph{Step 1, the base $B$.}  The $k-r$ blocks that $B$ misses lie in $S_0$, so
$B\cap E_{S_0}$ meets at least $\rho$ blocks, and it is independent in $M^{v}$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2),
\[
  \rho\le|B\cap E_{S_0}|\le r_{M^{v}}(E_{S_0})=\rho.
\]
So Step 5 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} applies to $B$: it gives
$g\in B\setminus E_{S_0}$ and $y\in E_{S_0}\setminus B$ in one cluster $C$ at scale
$\delta$ with $v_g<v_y$, and its last argument gives, for $f\in E_{S_0}\setminus B$,
\[
  v_b<v_f\qquad\bigl(b\in C(B,f)\setminus E_{S_0}\bigr).
\]

\emph{Step 2, a pair within $\delta$.}  Let
\[
  v_{z_2}=\min\{v_z:z\in C\cap E_{S_0},\ v_z>v_g\},\qquad
  v_{z_1}=\max\{v_z:z\in C\setminus E_{S_0},\ v_z<v_{z_2}\},
\]
the sets being nonempty by $y$ and $g$.  Then $v_g\le v_{z_1}<v_{z_2}$, no value
of $C$ lies in $(v_{z_1},v_{z_2})$, and as in Step 6 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:potential}, $v_{z_2}-v_{z_1}\le\delta$.  We find $x\in E_{S_0}$ and
$z\notin E_{S_0}$, one of them in $B$, with
\begin{equation}
  0<v_x-v_z\le\delta.
  \label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
If $z_2\in B$, take $(x,z)=(z_2,z_1)$.  Otherwise $z_2\in\PO_\delta$ lies in
$\cl(C(B,z_2)-z_2)$ but not in $\cl\{f:v_f<v_{z_2}-\delta\}$, so by the minimality
of $B$ some $b\in C(B,z_2)-z_2$ has
\[
  v_{z_2}-\delta\le v_b\le v_{z_2}.
\]
As $b\in B\subseteq\PO_\delta$ lies in the component of $z_2$ and within $\delta$
of it, $b\in C$, and \eqref{eq:pair} holds, with $x$ and $z$ in the cluster $C$ and so
in one component, for
\[
  (x,z)=
  \begin{cases}
    (z_2,b),&b\notin E_{S_0},\ \text{where }v_b<v_{z_2}\text{ by Step 1},\\
    (b,z_1),&b\in E_{S_0},\ v_b=v_{z_2},\\
    (z_2,g),&b\in E_{S_0},\ v_b<v_{z_2},
  \end{cases}
\]
in the last case because $v_b\le v_g$ by the choice of $z_2$, so that
$0<v_{z_2}-v_g\le v_{z_2}-v_b\le\delta$.

\emph{Step 3, item 1.}  By Steps 2 and 3 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} and \eqref{eq:pair},
\[
  v'_x-v'_z\le(v_x-\tau)-\bigl(v_z-(\tau-\delta)\bigr)=(v_x-v_z)-\delta\le0.
\]

\emph{Step 4, item 2.}  Let $\rho=0$, and suppose $\nu(M^{v'})=r$ and
$\Av(M^{v'})=S_0$.  By \eqref{eq:jump}, $r_{N(\delta)}(E_{S_0})>0$, so some
$y\in E_{S_0}$ lies in a base of $N(\delta)$, hence in $\PO_\delta$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2) for $M^{v'}$, $r_{M^{v'}}(E_{S_0})=\rho=0$, so a
minimum base $B'$ of $v'$ misses $E_{S_0}$, and the last argument of Step 1, at
$v'$ and $B'$, gives $v'_f<v'_y$ for $f\in C(B',y)-y$.  By Step 3 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:potential},
\[
  v_f\le v'_f+(\tau-\delta)<v'_y+(\tau-\delta)=v_y-\delta\qquad\bigl(f\in C(B',y)-y\bigr),
\]
so $y\in\cl\bigl(C(B',y)-y\bigr)\subseteq\cl\{f:v_f<v_y-\delta\}$, against
$y\in\PO_\delta$.

\emph{Step 5, counting.}  Call a maximal run of updates of stage $r$ that start
from states with the same $S_0$ an \emph{epoch}.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:potential}(1)
and Step 8 of its proof, $S_0$ grows strictly from one epoch to the next and
$0\le\rho\le r-1$, so the epochs have distinct values of $\rho$, and by item 2 the
epoch with $\rho=0$, if any, has one update.  In an epoch with $\rho\ge1$ fix $B$
at its first state; by Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}(2) it is a base of $M^{v}$
meeting $r$ blocks at every state of the epoch.  Let
\[
  Q(v)=\bigl|\{(x,z):x\in E_{S_0},\ z\notin E_{S_0},\ x\in B\text{ or }z\in B,\
  v_x>v_z\}\bigr|,
\]
formed with the $S_0$ of the epoch.  By Step 2 of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:potential} no pair with $v_x\le v_z$ returns to $v_x>v_z$, and by
item 1 every update of the epoch moves some pair counted by $Q$ to
$v'_x\le v'_z$, so $Q$ drops by at least one at every update, while
\[
  Q(v)\le|B\cap E_{S_0}|\,|E\setminus E_{S_0}|+|E_{S_0}|\,|B\setminus E_{S_0}|\le|B|\,m=km.
\]
So an epoch with $\rho\ge1$ has at most $km$ updates, and stage $r$ has at most
$1+(r-1)km$.
\end{proof}

When the components are as small as possible, the pair of item 1 ends the epoch at
once.

\begin{corollary}[Components of rank one]
\label{cor:rankone}
If every connected component of $M$ has rank at most one, every update of stage $r$
changes $S_0$ or ends the stage, and stage $r$ ends after at most $r$ updates.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, a circuit.}  Let $B$, $x$ and $z$ be as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:stagelength}(1).  Since $x\in E_{S_0}$ and $z\notin E_{S_0}$, $x\ne z$, and
as their component has rank one, $\{x,z\}$ is a circuit.

\emph{Step 2, $z\in B$.}  If $x\in B$, then $z\notin B$ and $C(B,z)=\{x,z\}$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1) would give
\[
  v_x\le v_z<v_x.
\]
So $z\in B$, $x\notin B$ and $C(B,x)=\{x,z\}$.

\emph{Step 3, the epoch ends.}  By Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}(2), $B$ is a minimum base
of $v'$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(1) and Theorem~\ref{thm:stagelength}(1),
\[
  v'_z\le v'_x\le v'_z,
\]
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:minbases}(3) $B-z+x$ is a minimum base of $v'$ with
\[
  |(B-z+x)\cap E_{S_0}|=|B\cap E_{S_0}|+1=\rho+1,
\]
by Step 1 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:stagelength}.  If $\nu(M^{v'})=r$ and
$\Av(M^{v'})=S_0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2) for $M^{v'}$ gives
$r_{M^{v'}}(E_{S_0})=\rho$, which this base exceeds.  So $\nu(M^{v'})>r$ or
$\Av(M^{v'})\supsetneq S_0$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:potential}(1).

\emph{Step 4, counting.}  By Step 8 of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:potential},
$k-r\le|S_0|\le k-1$ within the stage, and by Step 3 every update of the stage
that does not end it increases $|S_0|$.  So the stage has at most
$(k-1)-(k-r)+1=r$ updates.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The circuit}
\label{sec:circuit}

The observation behind an update is a question of unweighted matroid
intersection, and the augmenting path algorithm answers it without branching on
weights.

\begin{lemma}[Augmenting paths by a circuit]
\label{lem:augment}
Let a Boolean circuit of size $q$ decide, from input bits $x$ and the indicator
vector of a set $J\subseteq E$, whether $J$ is independent in a matroid $N_x$ on
$E$.  Then a Boolean circuit with at most $k(m^{2}q+8m^{3}+12m^{2}+12m+4)$ gates
computes from $x$ the indicator vector of a rainbow independent set of $N_x$ of
size $\nu(N_x)$.  For every $r_0\ge0$, a Boolean circuit of size at most
$(k+1)(m+1)^{2}(q+10(m+1))+5\lceil\log_2(r_0+1)\rceil$ computes from $x$ the binary
digits of $\nu(N_x)$ and the $k$ bits
$\mathbf 1[\nu(N_x)\le r_0]\wedge\mathbf 1[b\in\Av(N_x)]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, one round.}  From the indicator vector of a rainbow independent set
$I$, a round builds the exchange graph $D_I$ of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable} with $m(m-1)+m=m^{2}$ independence tests, of $I-x'+y$
for the arcs $y\to x'$ and of $I+y$ for the sinks, reading sources and arcs inside
blocks from $I$.  A breadth-first search from the sources, the sink of least index
in the first layer containing a sink, the predecessors of least index and the
exchange along this shortest path cost $8m^{3}+12m^{2}+12m+4$ gates, so a round
costs
\[
  m^{2}q+8m^{3}+12m^{2}+12m+4.
\]
By the exchange lemma for shortest paths~\cite{schrijver03} the new set is
rainbow, independent and has $|I|+1$ elements, and if no sink is reachable, $I$
is maximum and is kept~\cite{lawler75,schrijver03}.

\emph{Step 2, $k$ rounds.}  From $I=\varnothing$, $|I|$ grows by one per round
until $|I|=\nu(N_x)\le k$, so $k$ rounds give $|I|=\nu(N_x)$.

\emph{Step 3, the bits.}  One more exchange graph ($m(m-1)$ tests) and its
reachable set $R$ ($2m^{3}$ gates) give, by Steps 2 to 6 of the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable},
\[
  \Av(N_x)=\{b:b\text{ missed by }I\}\cup\blk(I\cap R).
\]
A population count of $I$, a comparison with $r_0$ and $k$ conjunctions complete
the outputs, within $(k+1)(m+1)^{2}(q+10(m+1))+5\lceil\log_2(r_0+1)\rceil$ gates.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Observer]
\label{lem:observer}
Let a Boolean circuit of size $s$ output the binary digits of $r(S)$ from the
indicator vector of $S\subseteq E$, and fix $r_0\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}$.  A Boolean
circuit of size $O(km^{3}(s+m))$ reads a relation $G\subseteq E\times E$ and
outputs $k$ bits $F_b(G)$, $b\in[k]$, such that
\[
  F_b(G_t)=\mathbf 1\bigl[\nu(N(t))\le r_0\bigr]\wedge\mathbf 1\bigl[b\in\Av(N(t))\bigr]
\]
for every $v\in\R^{E}$ and $t\ge0$, where $N(t)$ is the family of
Definition~\ref{def:posl} for $v$ and $G_t=\{(e,f):v_f-v_e\le t\}$.  Moreover
$F_b(E\times E)=0$ for every $b$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:posl}, $N(t)$ depends only on $G_t$; for $G=G_t$ write
$N_G=N(t)$, $\PO=\PO_t$ and $u=u_t$.  Steps 1 to 3 compute them from $G$,
correctly whenever $G=G_t$.

\emph{Step 1, the possibly optimal elements.}  For $f\in E$ let
$X_f=\{g:(g,f)\notin G\}$.  At $G=G_t$,
\[
  X_f=\{g:v_g<v_f-t\},\qquad f\in\PO\iff r(X_f+f)>r(X_f),
\]
which costs $m(2s+m+O(\log m))$ gates: $m$ negations, two rank evaluations and a
comparison for each $f$.

\emph{Step 2, the clusters.}  The components of $M$ are fixed.  The linkage bits
$[(x,y)\in G]\wedge[(y,x)\in G]$ for $x,y\in\PO$ in one component cost $O(m^{2})$
gates, and Warshall's algorithm~\cite{warshall62} closes them transitively with
$2m^{3}$ gates.  Since different clusters are more than $t$ apart, for $x,y$ in
one component
\[
  u(x)<u(y)\iff\bigl[x\in\PO,\ y\notin\PO\bigr]\vee
  \bigl[x,y\in\PO\text{ in different clusters},\ (x,y)\notin G\bigr],
\]
and the representative of a cluster is its element of least index, at $O(m^{2})$
gates.

\emph{Step 3, rank queries.}  Let $\mathrm{Lev}(x)$ be the cluster of a
representative $x$ and $\mathrm{Below}(x)$ the set of elements of its component
with smaller $u$.  By \eqref{eq:rank-mu} within components,
\[
  r_{N_G}(A)=\sum_{x}\Bigl(r\bigl(\mathrm{Below}(x)\cup(A\cap\mathrm{Lev}(x))\bigr)
  -r\bigl(\mathrm{Below}(x)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
the sum over the representatives; the top level $E\setminus\PO$ contributes $0$,
as $\PO\supseteq\PO_0$ spans $E$.  The ranks $r(\mathrm{Below}(x))$ cost $ms$
gates once, and then an independence test $[r_{N_G}(J)=|J|]$ costs
$q=ms+O(m^{2})$ gates.

\emph{Step 4, the intersection.}  Lemma~\ref{lem:augment} with input $G$, the
matroids $N_G$ and these tests gives the bits $F_b(G)$ with
\[
  (k+1)(m+1)^{2}\bigl(q+10(m+1)\bigr)+5\lceil\log_2(r_0+1)\rceil=O\bigl(km^{3}(s+m)\bigr)
\]
gates, and Steps 1 to 3 cost $O(m^{2}s+m^{3})$.

\emph{Step 5, the top level.}  $E\times E=G_t$ for $t\ge\max v-\min v$, where
$N(t)=M$ by Step 4 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:posl}, so
$\nu(N(t))=k>r_0$ and $F_b(E\times E)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One update]
\label{lem:oneupdate}
For each stage $r$, a signed tropical circuit of size $O(km^{5}(s+m))$ maps
$(w,p)$ to $p+a$, with $a$ the PO-SL update at $v=w-p_{\blk}$, at every state
with $\nu_0\ge r$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, the differences.}  The weights $v$ cost $m$ gates and the vector
$D^{+}\in\R^{E\times E}_{\ge0}$, $D^{+}_{ef}=\max(v_f-v_e,0)$, costs $2m^{2}$, with
\[
  \{(e,f):D^{+}_{ef}\le t\}=G_t\qquad(t\ge0).
\]

\emph{Step 2, the update as a threshold integral.}  Let $F_b$ be the bits of
Lemma~\ref{lem:observer} with $r_0=r$.  If $\nu_0=r$, then by
\eqref{eq:update-integral}, Proposition~\ref{prop:posl} and
$F_b(E\times E)=0$,
\[
  a_b=\int_0^{\infty}F_b(G_t)\,dt=\int_0^{\max D^{+}}F_b\bigl(G_t(D^{+})\bigr)\,dt=\CC_{F_b}(D^{+}),
\]
and if $\nu_0>r$, then $\nu(N(t))\ge\nu_0>r$ for all $t$ and
$\CC_{F_b}(D^{+})=0=a_b$.  The gap sum reads $F_b$ only at sublevel sets of
$D^{+}$, where the observer is correct, so Theorem~\ref{thm:compiler} on the
$m^{2}$ pairs with $N=k$ gives $a$ with at most
\[
  3m^{2}\cdot O\bigl(km^{3}(s+m)\bigr)+km^{2}+6m^{4}=O\bigl(km^{5}(s+m)\bigr)
\]
gates.

\emph{Step 3, the sum.}  $p+a$ costs $k$ gates.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
\emph{Step 1, the schedule.}  Start from $p=0$, where $\nu_0\ge1$ since a minimum
base of $w$ meets some block.  For $r=1,\ldots,k-1$ apply the circuit of
Lemma~\ref{lem:oneupdate} for stage $r$
\[
  \ell_r=\min\bigl(r(\lfloor m^{2}/4\rfloor+1),\,1+(r-1)km\bigr)
\]
times; at $\nu_0>r$ it is the identity, as $a=0$.

\emph{Step 2, the invariant.}  By induction on $r$, $\nu_0\ge r$ at the start of
stage $r$.  As $\nu_0$ never decreases (Theorem~\ref{thm:validity}(2)) and
Theorems~\ref{thm:potential} and~\ref{thm:stagelength} bound the updates at
$\nu_0=r$ by $\ell_r$, $\nu_0\ge r+1$ after stage $r$, and $\nu_0=k$ after stage
$k-1$.

\emph{Step 3, the output.}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:duality} and Theorem~\ref{thm:A},
at $O(ms+m^{2})$ further gates the circuit outputs
\[
  \Psi(p)=\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b+\beta_M(v)=\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_b-h_M(-v)=\Opt(w).
\]

\emph{Step 4, the size.}  The number of updates is at most
\[
  \sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\ell_r\le\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\bigl(1+(r-1)km\bigr)
  \le(k-1)\bigl(1+(k-2)km\bigr)\le k^{3}m,
\]
each of size $O(km^{5}(s+m))$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:oneupdate}, so the size is
$O(k^{4}m^{6}(s+m))$.  If every connected component of $M$ has rank at most one,
Corollary~\ref{cor:rankone} allows $\ell_r=r$ in Step 1, and
\[
  \sum_{r=1}^{k-1}r=\frac{k(k-1)}2\le k^{2}
\]
updates give the size $O(k^{3}m^{5}(s+m))$.  The circuit depends only on $M$, the
blocks and the rank circuit, so the family is uniform.
\end{proof}

\section{Two matroids}
\label{sec:two}

Common bases of two matroids are rainbow bases of a third one on twice as many
elements, with one block for the two copies of each element, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:C} applies to weighted matroid intersection without change.

\begin{lemma}[Doubling]
\label{lem:doubling}
Let $M_1,M_2$ be matroids of rank $\rho$ on $E$.  On $E\times\{1,2\}$ let
$N=M_1\oplus M_2^{*}$, with blocks $\{(e,1),(e,2)\}$ and weight $w_e$ on $(e,1)$,
$0$ on $(e,2)$.  For $X\subseteq E$, the set $X\times\{1\}\cup(E\setminus X)\times\{2\}$,
which meets every block once, is a base of $N$ if and only if $X$ is a common base
of $M_1$ and $M_2$, and its weight is $w(X)$.  Every rainbow base of $N$ has this
form.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, bases of $N$.}  Since the bases of $M_2^{*}$ are the complements of
the bases of $M_2$,
\[
  X\times\{1\}\cup(E\setminus X)\times\{2\}\in\Bases(N)
  \iff X\in\Bases(M_1),\ E\setminus X\in\Bases(M_2^{*})
  \iff X\in\Bases(M_1)\cap\Bases(M_2),
\]
and only the first copies carry weight, so the weight is $w(X)$.

\emph{Step 2, every rainbow base.}  $N$ has rank $\rho+(m-\rho)=m$, so a rainbow
base meets each of the $m$ blocks once, and it is the set of Step 1 for the set
$X$ of elements whose first copy it contains.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}]
\emph{Step 1, the instance.}  If $M_1$ and $M_2$ have a common base, both have
rank $\rho$, and $N$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling} has rank $m$ on $2m$ elements
with $m$ blocks, its rainbow bases corresponding to the common bases with equal
weights (Figure~\ref{fig:double}).

\emph{Step 2, its rank circuit.}  For $A,B\subseteq E$,
\[
  r_N\bigl(A\times\{1\}\cup B\times\{2\}\bigr)=r_1(A)+r_2^{*}(B)=r_1(A)+|B|+r_2(E\setminus B)-r_2(E),
\]
computed from the two rank circuits, the second on the negated indicator vector
of $B$, a population count, two additions and the subtraction of the constant
$r_2(E)$ on $O(\log m)$ bits, at $s_1+s_2+O(m)$ gates.

\emph{Step 3, common bases.}  Theorem~\ref{thm:C} with $2m$ elements, $k=m$
blocks and the weights of Lemma~\ref{lem:doubling} gives the minimum weight of a
common base with
\[
  O\bigl(m^{4}(2m)^{6}(s_1+s_2+m)\bigr)=O\bigl(m^{10}(s_1+s_2+m)\bigr)
\]
gates.

\emph{Step 4, common independent sets.}  Let $\nu_{12}$ be the largest size of a
common independent set.  For $j\le\nu_{12}$ the truncations $M_i^{(j)}$, with rank
functions $\min(r_i,j)$ and rank circuits of size $s_i+O(\log m)$, satisfy
\[
  \Bases\bigl(M_1^{(j)}\bigr)\cap\Bases\bigl(M_2^{(j)}\bigr)
  =\{X:X\text{ common independent},\ |X|=j\}.
\]
So Step 3 for the truncations and the weights $-w$ gives
\[
  c_j=-\max\{w(X):X\text{ common independent},\ |X|=j\},
\]
and $\max\{w(X):X\text{ common independent}\}=\max(0,-c_1,\ldots,-c_{\nu_{12}})$, at
$O(m^{11}(s_1+s_2+m))$ gates.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Transversal matroids and gammoids.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:augment} computes the largest size of a rainbow independent set,
that is, it solves unweighted matroid intersection with a partition matroid.
Transversal matroids and gammoids have rank functions of this kind, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} applies to them.

\begin{corollary}[Transversal matroids and gammoids]
\label{cor:transversal}
The following matroids $M$ on $m$ elements have Boolean rank circuits and signed
tropical circuits for $h_M$ of polynomial size.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let $A_1,\ldots,A_n\subseteq E$, $|E|=m$, and $p=|A_1|+\cdots+|A_n|\le mn$.
        The partial transversals, the sets $X\subseteq E$ with an injective map
        $\phi:X\to[n]$ such that $x\in A_{\phi(x)}$ for every $x\in X$, are the
        independent sets of a matroid $M$, the transversal matroid.  It has a
        Boolean rank circuit of size at most $36(n+1)(p+1)^{2}(m+p+1)$, and $h_M$
        has a signed tropical circuit of size at most
        $120(m+1)(n+1)(p+1)^{2}(m+p+1)\le120(m+1)^{4}(n+1)^{4}$.
  \item Let $D$ be a directed graph on a set $V$ of $v$ vertices whose $a$ arcs are
        ordered pairs of vertices, and let $S,E\subseteq V$ with $|E|=m$.  A set
        $X\subseteq E$ is \emph{linked} from $S$ if there are directed paths, one
        from a vertex of $S$ to each $x\in X$, no two of which share a vertex; a
        vertex of $S\cap X$ is linked by the path without arcs.  The sets linked
        from $S$ are the independent sets of a matroid $M$, the gammoid.  It has a
        Boolean rank circuit of size at most $48(v+1)(v+a+1)^{2}(2v+a+1)$, and
        $h_M$ has a signed tropical circuit of size at most
        $160(m+1)(v+1)(v+a+1)^{2}(2v+a+1)\le160(v+1)^{8}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1, transversal matroids as intersections.}  For $S\subseteq E$ let
$N_S$ be the matroid on the $p$ incidences $(e,i)$, $e\in A_i$, in which a set is
independent when it contains at most one incidence of each element and only
incidences of elements of $S$, with the blocks $R_i=\{(e,i):e\in A_i\}$.  Rainbow
independent sets of $N_S$ are matchings of elements of $S$ into distinct sets,
so
\[
  \max\{|X|:X\subseteq S\text{ a partial transversal}\}=\nu(N_S).
\]

\emph{Step 2, the matroid property.}  Subsets of partial transversals are partial
transversals.  Let $X,Y$ be partial transversals such that $X+y$ is none for
$y\in Y\setminus X$, let $Q=\Av(N_X)$ and $E_Q$ the incidences in the blocks of
$Q$, and put $A_Q=\bigcup_{i\in Q}A_i$.  An element $y\in Y\cap A_Q$ lies in $X$,
since a matching of $X$ missing the set $A_i\ni y$, $i\in Q$, would extend to
$X+y$.  Hence
\[
  r_{N_Y}(E_Q)=|Y\cap A_Q|\le|X\cap A_Q|=r_{N_X}(E_Q),
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:avoidable}(2) for $N_X$ and~(1) for $N_Y$, with
$\nu(N_X)=|X|$ and $\nu(N_Y)=|Y|$ by Step 1, give
\[
  n-|X|=|Q|-r_{N_X}(E_Q)\le|Q|-r_{N_Y}(E_Q)\le n-|Y|.
\]
So the partial transversals form a matroid, as Edmonds and Fulkerson
showed~\cite{oxley11}, with rank function $r(S)=\nu(N_S)$ by Step 1.

\emph{Step 3, the transversal rank circuit.}  The set $J$ is independent in
$N_S$ if and only if
\[
  |J|=r_{N_S}(J)=\bigl|\{e\in S:(e,i)\in J\text{ for some }i\}\bigr|,
\]
a test of at most $28p+23m+1$ gates with the bits of $S$ as extra inputs: one
disjunction per incidence, one conjunction per element and two population
counts.  Lemma~\ref{lem:augment} on the $p$ incidences and $n$ blocks and a
population count give $r(S)$ within $36(n+1)(p+1)^{2}(m+p+1)$ gates.
Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $k\le m$ gives the second bound, and $p\le mn$ gives
$p+1,\ m+p+1\le(m+1)(n+1)$ and the last one.

\emph{Step 4, gammoids as duals.}  Let $T$ be the transversal matroid on $V$ of
$A_w=\{w\}\cup\{u:u\to w\}$, $w\in V\setminus S$.  As $V\setminus S$ is a
transversal, the bases of $T$ are the sets $B$ with a bijection
$f:B\to V\setminus S$ such that $b\in A_{f(b)}$.  We show, with Ingleton and
Piff~\cite{oxley11},
\[
  X\text{ is linked from }S\iff X\cap B=\varnothing\text{ for some }B\in\Bases(T)
  \iff X\text{ is independent in }T^{*}.
\]
Let $X\cap B=\varnothing$ and $g=f^{-1}$.  For $x\in X\setminus S$ the sequence
$x,g(x),g^{2}(x),\ldots$, continued while it stays outside $S$, lies in $B$ after
its first term and is injective, as $g$ is injective and $x\notin g(V\setminus S)$;
so it reaches $S$, and $g(y)\in A_y-y$ gives arcs $g(y)\to y$.  These paths are
disjoint by the injectivity of $g$.  Conversely, for paths linking $X$ from $S$,
each starting at its last vertex in $S$, the vertices of the paths other than
their last ones, sent to their successors, and the vertices outside $S$ on no
path, sent to themselves, form such a pair $(B,f)$ with $X\cap B=\varnothing$.
So the gammoid is the restriction of $T^{*}$ to $E$.

\emph{Step 5, the gammoid rank circuit.}  The family $(A_w)$ has at most $v$ sets
and $v+a$ incidences, so Step 3 for $T$, the dual circuit of
Corollary~\ref{cor:classes}(4) on $V$, and the input bits placed on $E$ ($0$ on
$V\setminus E$) give a rank circuit with
\[
  36(v+1)(v+a+1)^{2}(2v+a+1)+(v+1)+11(v+1)\lceil\log_2(v+1)\rceil
  \le48(v+1)(v+a+1)^{2}(2v+a+1)
\]
gates.  Theorem~\ref{thm:A} with $k\le m\le v$ and $a\le v^{2}$ gives the other
two bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and open problems}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Stage length.}  Theorems~\ref{thm:potential} and~\ref{thm:stagelength}
bound stage $r$ by $\min\bigl(r(\lfloor m^{2}/4\rfloor+1),\,1+(r-1)km\bigr)$
updates.  The companion paper's bound of $r+1$ updates per stage does not hold
for PO-SL, and no bound in terms of $r$ and $k$ alone does, while the exact
family $\Pos_t$, which is not admissible, needs one update on the same input.

\begin{proposition}[Halving ladder]
\label{prop:ladder}
For $L\ge0$ let $M$ be the cycle matroid of the multigraph on the vertices
$0,1,2,3$ with the edges, blocks and weights
\[
  \begin{array}{c|cccccc}
    & g' & g & b_i\ (1\le i\le L) & h & h' & y\\\hline
    \text{edge} & 03 & 02 & 02 & 01 & 01 & 12\\
    \text{block} & R_2 & R_2 & R_2 & R_1 & R_3 & R_1\\
    \text{weight} & 0 & 0 & 2^{L-i} & 2^{L} & 2^{L} & 2^{L}+1
  \end{array}
\]
so that $k=3$ and $m=L+5$.  Stage $2$ of PO-SL takes exactly $L+2=m-3$ updates.
With $\Pos_t$ in place of $N(t)$ in Definition~\ref{def:update}, $\nu(t)$ read as
the largest number of blocks met by a base in $\Pos_t$, stage $2$ takes one
update.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $A_j=2^{L-j}$ for $0\le j\le L$ and $A_{L+1}=0$, and let \emph{state $j$} be
the weights with
\[
  v_h=v_{h'}=A_j,\qquad v_y=A_j+1,
\]
and $v_e=w_e$ on the other edges, so that state $0$ is $p=0$.

\emph{Step 1, the stage.}  The edge $g'$ is the only one at vertex $3$, so it
lies in every spanning tree, and $T=\{g',y,h'\}$ is the only rainbow spanning
tree.  At every state $j$ the minimum spanning trees are $\{g',g,h\}$ and
$\{g',g,h'\}$, so $\nu_0=2$, $S_0=\{1,3\}$, and stage $1$ has no updates.  No
base misses $R_2$, so $\lambda_2=\infty$, and every update lowers $h,h',y$ by
$\tau$ and fixes the other values.

\emph{Step 2, possibly optimal edges.}  At state $j$ the edges $g',g,h,h'$ lie in
minimum spanning trees, hence in $\PO_t$, and as $b_i$ is parallel to $g$ and $y$
closes the triangle $\{g,h,y\}$,
\[
  b_i\in\PO_t\iff t\ge v_{b_i}-v_g=2^{L-i},\qquad
  y\in\PO_t\iff t\ge v_y-v_h=1.
\]

\emph{Step 3, states $j<L$.}  Here $A_j\ge2$.  For $t<A_j/2$ every $b_i\in\PO_t$
satisfies $v_{b_i}\le t$, so
\[
  v_h-v_{b_i}\ge A_j-t>\frac{A_j}2>t,
\]
and the clusters of the triangle are $\{g\}\cup\{b_i\in\PO_t\}$ and
$\{h,h'\}\cup(\{y\}\cap\PO_t)$.  A base of $N(t)$ then consists of $g'$, an edge
$02$ and one of $h,h',y$, so $\nu(N(t))=2$ and $\Av(N(t))=\{1,3\}$.  At
$t=A_j/2$ the edge $b_{j+1}$, of value $A_j/2$, enters $\PO_t$, and
\[
  |v_g-v_{b_{j+1}}|=|v_{b_{j+1}}-v_h|=\frac{A_j}2=t,\qquad|v_h-v_y|=1\le t
\]
join one cluster, so $T$ is a base of $N(t)$.  Hence $\tau=A_j/2$, and the update
leads to state $j+1$, as $A_j-A_j/2=A_{j+1}$.

\emph{Step 4, states $L$ and $L+1$.}  Here $v_g=0$ and $v_h=v_{h'}=A_j\le1$.  For
$t<1$ the triangle meets $\PO_t$ in $\{g,h,h'\}$, as $v_{b_i}\ge1$, so a base of
$N(t)$ consists of $g'$, $g$ and one of $h,h'$, and $\nu(N(t))=2$,
$\Av(N(t))=\{1,3\}$.  At $t=1$ the edge $y$ enters $\PO_t$, and $|v_g-v_h|\le1$,
$|v_h-v_y|=1$ join one cluster, so $\tau=1$.  The update leads from state $L$ to
state $L+1$, and from state $L+1$ to
\[
  v_h=v_{h'}=-1,\qquad v_y=0,\qquad v(T)=0+0-1=\min_{B\in\Bases(M)}v(B),
\]
where $\nu_0=3$.  So stage $2$ takes $L$ updates in Step 3 and two in Step 4.

\emph{Step 5, the exact family.}  At state $0$ the fundamental circuits of $T$ are
$\{g,h',y\}$, $\{h,h'\}$ and $\{b_i,h',y\}$, so
\[
  \theta(T)=v_y-v_g=2^{L}+1,
\]
and as $T$ is the only rainbow spanning tree, the least $t$ at which a base in
$\Pos_t$ meets three blocks is $\tau=2^{L}+1$.  As in Step 1, $\lambda_1=\lambda_3=0$
and $\lambda_2=\infty$, so the update lowers $h,h',y$ by $2^{L}+1$, to
\[
  v_h=v_{h'}=-1,\qquad v_y=0,
\]
where $T$ is minimum, as in Step 4, and $\nu_0=3$.
\end{proof}

Two questions are open.  Does a stage of PO-SL take $O(rm)$ updates, or can it
take $\Theta(rkm)$?  Is there an admissible family, read from $G_t$, whose stages
take $O(r)$ updates?

\paragraph{Size.}  The bounds of Theorems~\ref{thm:C} and \ref{thm:D} are not
tuned.  For the assignment problem, a partition matroid whose components have
rank one, Theorem~\ref{thm:C} with Corollary~\ref{cor:rankone} gives $O(n^{15})$
against the $O(n^{8})$ of the companion paper, whose specialized schedule and
observer we do not reuse.

\paragraph{The exact family.}  Proposition~\ref{prop:nphard} concerns
arbitrary states.  Whether rainbow questions about $\Pos_t$ stay NP-hard on the
states a schedule reaches is open; on such states the family still lacks a
min-max formula of Rado type, as small graphic examples show.

\paragraph{Beyond matroid intersection.}  Weighted matching in general graphs is
an instance of matroid parity, where the dual has no fixed index set; whether a
schedule of threshold integrals reaches it is open, as are lower bounds of any
super-polynomial kind for signed tropical circuits computing polynomial-time
optimization values.
